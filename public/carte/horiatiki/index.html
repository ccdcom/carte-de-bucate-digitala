🇬🇷 Greece

Horiatiki

Greek Village Salad
Rustic version ↓ Michelin version ↓

The story behind the dish

The village salad is Greece in a bowl: sun-ripe tomatoes, cucumber, onion, green pepper, Kalamata olives and a thick slab of feta, dressed with olive oil and dried oregano. No lettuce, no vinegar fuss, no chopping into tiny pieces.

It became a national icon only in the 20th century, when summer tourism carried it from village tavernas to the world. Its rule is simple: the ingredients must be perfect, because there is nowhere to hide. The best part is the juice at the bottom, mopped up with bread.

THE RUSTIC VERSION

The Island Taverna Salad

Serves 4 · 15 minutes
Chunky, generous and made only in summer, when tomatoes taste of the sun.

INGREDIENTS

  • 4 ripe tomatoes, cut into wedges
  • 1 cucumber, partly peeled, in thick half-moons
  • 1 small red onion, thinly sliced
  • 1 green bell pepper, in rings
  • A handful of Kalamata olives
  • 1 tbsp capers (optional)
  • One 7-oz slab of Greek feta
  • ⅓ cup good extra-virgin olive oil
  • 1 tsp dried Greek oregano, salt
  • Crusty bread, to serve

METHOD

  1. Put the tomatoes, cucumber, onion and pepper in a wide shallow bowl. Season lightly with salt and toss gently.
  2. Scatter the olives and capers over the top.
  3. Lay the feta slab on top, whole.
  4. Pour the olive oil generously over everything and crumble the oregano over the feta.
  5. Bring to the table with bread, and break the feta into the salad as you eat.
Tip. Never refrigerate the tomatoes. Salt them a few minutes ahead so they release their juice.
THE MICHELIN 3-STAR VERSION

Horiatiki, Clear and Cold

Serves 4 · 1 day (mostly resting)
Heirloom tomatoes in their own clear water, cucumber granita, whipped feta, olive soil and oregano oil.

INGREDIENTS

  • 3 lb very ripe tomatoes for tomato water, plus 1 lb mixed heirloom and cherry tomatoes
  • 2 cucumbers, juiced, with 1 tbsp sugar and a pinch of salt
  • 5 oz barrel-aged feta, ¼ cup Greek yogurt, 2 tbsp olive oil
  • ½ cup Kalamata olives, pitted and dried at 175 °F for 3 hours
  • Oregano oil: 1 bunch fresh oregano, ½ cup olive oil
  • 1 small red onion, pickled in red wine vinegar
  • Caper berries, purslane, flaky salt, best olive oil

TECHNIQUE

  1. Blend the 3 lb tomatoes with a pinch of salt, hang in a cloth over a bowl in the fridge overnight. Collect the clear tomato water.
  2. Cucumber granita: stir the sugar and salt into the cucumber juice, freeze in a flat tray, scraping with a fork every 30 minutes until flaky.
  3. Whip the feta with the yogurt and oil until smooth and airy.
  4. Chop the dried olives into a dark crumble.
  5. Blend the oregano with the oil, warm to 140 °F, and strain.
  6. Peel the heirloom tomatoes if thick-skinned, cut into wedges and season with flaky salt and olive oil.
In a chilled shallow bowl: a quenelle of whipped feta, tomato wedges arranged around it, pickled onion petals, caper berries and purslane, a line of olive crumble. At the table, pour the cold tomato water around and finish with a spoon of cucumber granita and drops of oregano oil.
