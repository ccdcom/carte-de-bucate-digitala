🇧🇬 Bulgaria

Lutenitsa

Roasted Red Pepper Spread
Rustic version ↓ Michelin version ↓

The story behind the dish

Every autumn, Bulgarian courtyards fill with the smell of roasting peppers. Families gather to peel them, grind them with tomatoes and eggplant, and cook the mixture for hours in wide pans, stirring until it turns thick and deep red. Then it goes into jars, to last until spring.

Lutenitsa (the name comes from lyut, “spicy”) is spread on warm bread with white cheese for breakfast, served beside grilled meats and eaten straight from the jar by children. Each family seasons it a little differently.

THE RUSTIC VERSION

Autumn Courtyard Lutenitsa

Makes about 4 cups · 3 hours
Roasted peppers, tomatoes and eggplant, slowly cooked until thick and sweet.

INGREDIENTS

  • 4 lb red bell peppers (or kapia peppers)
  • 1 medium eggplant
  • 2 lb ripe tomatoes
  • 1–2 hot chili peppers, to taste
  • ½ cup sunflower oil
  • 4 garlic cloves, crushed
  • 1 tbsp sugar
  • 2 tsp salt
  • 1 tbsp white wine vinegar
  • 1 tsp ground black pepper and 1 tbsp chopped parsley

METHOD

  1. Roast the peppers, eggplant and chili under a hot broiler or on a grill, turning, until blackened all over. Cover them in a bowl for 15 minutes, then peel and seed the peppers and scoop out the eggplant flesh.
  2. Score, blanch and peel the tomatoes.
  3. Grind or blend the peppers, eggplant, chili and tomatoes into a coarse purée.
  4. Pour into a wide heavy pot with the oil and cook over low heat for 1½–2 hours, stirring often, until thick enough that a spoon leaves a clear path.
  5. Stir in the garlic, sugar, salt, vinegar and black pepper and cook 10 more minutes. Add the parsley.
  6. Serve at room temperature with bread and white cheese, or pour hot into sterilized jars and seal.
THE MICHELIN 3-STAR VERSION

Lutenitsa, Ember and Sheep’s Curd

Serves 6 as a starter · 4 hours
A concentrated ember-roasted pepper paste, whipped sheep’s curd, charred pepper skin powder and a crisp of rye.

INGREDIENTS

  • 3 lb red kapia or Romano peppers, roasted over wood embers
  • 1 small eggplant, charred
  • 1 lb San Marzano or plum tomatoes, halved and slow-roasted at 250 °F for 2 hours
  • ⅓ cup cold-pressed sunflower oil
  • 1 roasted garlic head, 1 tsp smoked paprika, sherry vinegar, salt
  • Curd: 7 oz fresh sheep’s cheese or ricotta, ¼ cup thick yogurt, lemon zest
  • Rye crisps: thin slices of dark rye, brushed with oil
  • To finish: pepper-skin powder (dried skins ground fine), purslane or parsley leaves, chili oil

TECHNIQUE

  1. Peel the roasted peppers, saving the skins; scrape the eggplant flesh.
  2. Dry the pepper skins in a 175 °F oven for 2 hours and grind into a fine smoky powder.
  3. Blend the peppers, eggplant, roasted tomatoes and roasted garlic until smooth, then cook gently with the oil for 1½ hours, until reduced to a glossy, spoon-coating paste. Season with smoked paprika, vinegar and salt. Pass through a sieve.
  4. Whip the sheep’s cheese with the yogurt and lemon zest until airy. Season.
  5. Bake the rye slices at 300 °F for 20 minutes, until crisp.
A generous swirl of lutenitsa on a warm plate, a quenelle of whipped curd on top, a dusting of pepper-skin powder, drops of chili oil, a few purslane leaves and a standing shard of rye crisp.
