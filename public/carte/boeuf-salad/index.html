Boeuf Salad

Rustic version ↓ Michelin version ↓

The story behind the dish

Boeuf salad descends from the famous Olivier salad, created in 1860 by the Belgian chef Lucien Olivier at the Hermitage restaurant in Moscow. When the original recipe was lost, cooks across Eastern Europe rebuilt it with what they had, and Romania made it with beef.

Finely diced vegetables, tender beef, pickles and homemade mayonnaise: it is the dish of Christmas, New Year and Easter, decorated with olives, pickles and dill, and every family claims its version is the right one.

THE RUSTIC VERSION

The Holiday Platter

Serves 8 as a starter · 2½ hours (plus chilling)
Cut small and evenly, bound with real mayonnaise, decorated the way it has been for generations.

INGREDIENTS

  • 1 lb beef (brisket or round), or chicken breast
  • 1 onion, 1 carrot and 1 celery stalk for the broth
  • 3 medium potatoes
  • 3 carrots
  • 1 small celery root
  • 1 cup frozen green peas
  • 6 dill pickles, plus pickled red pepper (gogoșari)
  • Mayonnaise: 2 egg yolks, 1 tsp mustard, 1 cup sunflower oil, juice of ½ lemon, salt
  • 1–2 tbsp mustard to season the salad
  • To decorate: black olives, pickle slices, dill, a little extra mayonnaise

METHOD

  1. Simmer the beef with the onion, carrot and celery stalk in salted water for about 2 hours, until tender. Cool in the broth.
  2. Boil the potatoes, carrots and celery root separately until just tender. Cook the peas for 3 minutes. Let everything cool completely.
  3. Make the mayonnaise: whisk the yolks with the mustard and a pinch of salt, then add the oil drop by drop, then in a thin stream, until thick. Finish with lemon juice.
  4. Cut the beef, vegetables and pickles into small, even cubes about the size of a pea. Squeeze excess liquid from the pickles and celery.
  5. Mix everything with two-thirds of the mayonnaise and the mustard. Taste and season with salt and pepper.
  6. Shape on a platter, smooth the top with the remaining mayonnaise, and decorate with olives, pickles, pepper strips and dill. Chill for at least 2 hours.
Tip. The salad is only as good as the dice: take your time and cut everything the same size.
THE MICHELIN 3-STAR VERSION

Olivier, Rewritten

Serves 6 as a starter · 5 hours
A precise cylinder of brunoise vegetables and confit beef tongue, light mustard emulsion, pickle gel and a quail egg.

INGREDIENTS

  • 1 veal or beef tongue, about 1½ lb, with 1 onion, 1 carrot, bay leaf and peppercorns
  • 1 Yukon Gold potato, 1 carrot, ¼ celery root, ⅓ cup fresh peas
  • 2 cornichons and 1 tbsp capers, finely chopped
  • Emulsion: 1 egg yolk, 1 tsp Dijon mustard, ½ cup grapeseed oil, 2 tbsp crème fraîche, lemon juice
  • Pickle gel: ½ cup pickle brine, ½ tsp agar-agar
  • 6 quail eggs
  • To finish: dill oil, chive tips, crisp potato threads, flaky salt

TECHNIQUE

  1. Simmer the tongue with the aromatics for 3 hours, until tender. Peel while warm, chill, and cut into ⅛-inch brunoise.
  2. Cut the potato, carrot and celery root into ⅛-inch dice. Blanch each separately until just tender, and the peas for 1 minute. Chill in ice water and dry well.
  3. Make a light mayonnaise with the yolk, mustard and oil, then fold in the crème fraîche and lemon juice.
  4. Pickle gel: boil the brine with the agar for 1 minute, set in the fridge, then blend until smooth and glossy.
  5. Boil the quail eggs for 2 minutes 20 seconds, chill and peel; halve them.
  6. Bind the tongue, vegetables, cornichons and capers with just enough emulsion to hold. Season.
Press the salad into a 2½-inch ring in the center of a cold plate and lift the ring. Glaze the top with a thin layer of emulsion, set a halved quail egg on it, dot pickle gel and dill oil around, and finish with chive tips and a nest of crisp potato threads.
