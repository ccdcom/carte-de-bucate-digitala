🇭🇺 Hungary

Foie Gras Tokaji

Rustic version ↓ Michelin version ↓

The story behind the dish

Hungary is one of the world’s great producers of goose and duck liver, and for generations it was farm food, cooked simply and sold at markets, long before it reached white tablecloths. Hungarian grandmothers still fry it in its own fat with onions and paprika.

The refined version pairs it with the country’s most famous wine, Tokaji Aszú, the golden sweet wine Louis XIV is said to have called “the wine of kings, the king of wines.” Its sweetness and acidity wrap the rich liver in a thin amber glaze.

THE RUSTIC VERSION

Farmhouse Goose Liver

Serves 4 as a starter · 30 minutes
Goose liver gently cooked in its own fat with onion and paprika, the way Hungarian farm cooks do it.

INGREDIENTS

  • 1 lb goose or duck foie gras (or fattened goose liver), cut into thick slices
  • 1 cup goose fat
  • 1 onion, thinly sliced
  • 2 garlic cloves, sliced
  • 1 tsp sweet Hungarian paprika
  • Salt and black pepper
  • Fresh bread, red onion rings and pickled peppers, to serve

METHOD

  1. Warm the goose fat in a small heavy pan over low heat.
  2. Add the onion and garlic and cook gently for 10 minutes, until soft.
  3. Add the liver slices and cook in the barely bubbling fat for 8–10 minutes, until just cooked through but still soft. Do not let the fat get too hot.
  4. Lift out the liver, season with salt and pepper and sprinkle with paprika.
  5. Serve warm on bread with red onion and pickled peppers. (Covered in its fat in a jar, it keeps for a week in the fridge.)
THE MICHELIN 3-STAR VERSION

Foie Gras Torchon, Tokaji Aszú

Serves 8 as a starter · 3 days
A silky foie gras torchon under a thin Tokaji Aszú glaze, with apricot, toasted brioche and walnut.

INGREDIENTS

  • 1 grade A goose or duck foie gras (about 1¼ lb), deveined
  • 1½ tsp salt, ½ tsp white pepper, ¼ tsp sugar, 2 tbsp Tokaji Aszú
  • Tokaji glaze: 1 cup Tokaji Aszú (5 puttonyos), 1 tbsp sugar, ½ tsp agar-agar
  • Apricot: 6 ripe apricots, poached in Tokaji; apricot purée
  • Brioche, cut into thin slices and toasted
  • Toasted walnuts, fleur de sel, micro herbs

TECHNIQUE

  1. Season the deveined foie gras with salt, pepper, sugar and Tokaji and marinate overnight.
  2. Roll it tightly in plastic wrap and then in a cloth into a firm 2-inch log, tying the ends. Poach in water at 160 °F for 8 minutes, then plunge into ice water.
  3. Re-tighten the log and hang in the fridge for 2 days.
  4. Boil the Tokaji with sugar and agar, cool slightly. Unwrap the torchon, slice it, and brush the slices with a thin layer of glaze, letting it set.
  5. Poach the apricots briefly in Tokaji; blend some into a purée.
A thick slice of torchon glistening under its amber glaze, a poached apricot half, dots of apricot purée, crushed toasted walnuts, a few flakes of salt, and warm thin brioche on the side.
