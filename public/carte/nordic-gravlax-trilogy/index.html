🇸🇪 Sweden

Nordic Gravlax Trilogy

Rustic version ↓ Michelin version ↓

The story behind the dish

Gravlax means “buried salmon”: medieval fishermen salted the fish and buried it in the sand above the high-tide line, where it fermented. Later it was cured with salt, sugar and dill, and became the elegant classic of every Swedish smörgåsbord.

This trilogy shows three expressions of the cure: the classic dill, a juniper-infused version with the resin of the northern forests, and a ruby beetroot cure. All are served with sweet mustard-dill sauce (hovmästarsås).

THE RUSTIC VERSION

Classic Dill Gravlax

Serves 8 as a starter · 30 minutes, plus 2–3 days curing
Salmon cured for two days with salt, sugar and dill, with mustard sauce.

INGREDIENTS

  • 2 lb very fresh salmon fillet, skin on, pin bones removed (previously frozen for safety)
  • ¼ cup coarse salt
  • ¼ cup sugar
  • 1 tbsp white peppercorns, crushed
  • A large bunch of dill, chopped
  • Mustard sauce: 3 tbsp Swedish or Dijon mustard, 1 tbsp sugar, 1 tbsp white wine vinegar, ⅓ cup oil, 3 tbsp chopped dill
  • Rye bread and boiled potatoes, to serve

METHOD

  1. Cut the fillet in half crosswise.
  2. Mix the salt, sugar and pepper. Rub it over the flesh of both pieces and cover one with the dill.
  3. Lay the second piece on top, flesh to flesh, like a sandwich. Wrap tightly in plastic wrap, put in a dish with a weight on top and refrigerate for 2–3 days, turning every 12 hours.
  4. Mustard sauce: whisk the mustard, sugar and vinegar, then the oil in a thin stream. Stir in the dill.
  5. Scrape off the cure, pat dry and slice thinly on a slant, away from the skin.
  6. Serve with the mustard sauce, rye bread and potatoes.
THE MICHELIN 3-STAR VERSION

Three Cures of Salmon

Serves 6 as a starter · 3 days
Dill gravlax, juniper-and-gin gravlax and beetroot gravlax, with mustard-honey sauce, pickled cucumber and warm buttered brioche.

INGREDIENTS

  • 1½ lb center-cut salmon fillet (previously frozen), cut into three equal pieces
  • Base cure: ½ cup coarse salt, ½ cup sugar, divided in three
  • Dill cure: dill, white pepper
  • Juniper cure: 10 crushed juniper berries, 2 tbsp gin, lemon zest
  • Beetroot cure: 1 raw beet, grated, 1 tbsp horseradish, orange zest
  • Mustard-honey sauce, cucumber ribbons pickled in vinegar and sugar, warm buttered brioche, dill and wood sorrel

TECHNIQUE

  1. Mix each third of the base cure with its aromatics. Coat each salmon piece with its own cure, wrap separately and refrigerate for 48 hours.
  2. Rinse, dry and chill uncovered for a few hours to firm.
  3. Slice each very thinly and evenly, or cut into neat rectangles.
  4. Toast slices of brioche in butter just before serving.
Three rolls or neat rectangles in a line, pale pink, deeper pink with juniper and vivid ruby beetroot, each with a dot of mustard-honey sauce, ribbons of pickled cucumber, dill and wood sorrel, with warm brioche on the side.
