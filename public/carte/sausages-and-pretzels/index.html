EUROPE ON A PLATE · RECIPE 55
🇩🇪 Germany

Sausages and Pretzels

Heritage on a Wooden Board
Rustic version ↓ Michelin version ↓

The story behind the dish

Germany counts more than 1,500 kinds of sausage: Thuringian and Franconian bratwurst grilled over charcoal, Munich’s white Weisswurst eaten before noon with sweet mustard, tiny Nürnberger, Berlin’s currywurst. Each region guards its spice blend.

The pretzel, or Brezel, its twisted arms said to imitate arms folded in prayer, gets its dark, glossy crust and unique flavor from a quick dip in a lye bath before baking. Together, on a wooden board with mustard and beer, they are the soul of a Bavarian beer garden.

THE RUSTIC VERSION

A Beer Garden Board

Serves 4 · 2 hours
Homemade soft pretzels, grilled bratwurst, sweet mustard and Obatzda cheese spread.

INGREDIENTS

  • Pretzels: 3½ cups bread flour, 2¼ tsp instant yeast, 1¼ cups warm water, 2 tbsp soft butter, 1 tsp sugar, 1½ tsp salt
  • Baking soda bath: 8 cups water, ½ cup baking soda (or food-grade lye, see tip)
  • Coarse pretzel salt
  • 8 bratwurst sausages
  • Obatzda: 7 oz ripe Camembert, 2 tbsp soft butter, 2 tbsp cream cheese, 1 tsp sweet paprika, ½ small onion, finely chopped, caraway, chives
  • Sweet Bavarian mustard, sauerkraut, radishes

METHOD

  1. Knead the pretzel ingredients for 8 minutes, cover and rest 30 minutes.
  2. Divide into 8 pieces. Roll each into a 24-inch rope, thick in the middle and thin at the ends. Form a U, cross the ends twice and press them onto the thick part.
  3. Chill uncovered for 30 minutes so the skin dries.
  4. Bring the water and baking soda to a simmer. Dip each pretzel for 30 seconds, drain, place on a lined sheet and sprinkle with salt. Slash the thick part.
  5. Bake at 425 °F for 15–18 minutes, until deep brown.
  6. Mash the Camembert with the butter, cream cheese, paprika, onion and caraway; top with chives.
  7. Grill the bratwurst over medium heat for 12–15 minutes, turning, until browned. Serve everything on a board with mustard, sauerkraut and radishes.
Tip. Bakeries use a 3–4% food-grade lye solution for the classic dark crust. It is caustic: wear gloves and eye protection. The baking soda bath is safer and works well.
THE MICHELIN 3-STAR VERSION

Wurst and Brezel, Elevated

Serves 4 · 1 day
A house-made veal and pork sausage, pretzel brioche, beer-mustard emulsion, sauerkraut purée and crisp pork skin.

INGREDIENTS

  • Sausage: 12 oz veal shoulder and 8 oz pork back fat, very cold, ground fine, with crushed ice, salt, white pepper, mace, lemon zest, parsley; natural casings
  • Pretzel brioche rolls: brioche dough, dipped in a baking soda bath and baked with pretzel salt
  • Beer-mustard emulsion: 2 tbsp sweet mustard, 2 tbsp grainy mustard, ¼ cup reduced wheat beer, ¼ cup grapeseed oil
  • Sauerkraut purée: 1 cup sauerkraut, braised with apple and white wine, blended with butter
  • Crisp pork skin (puffed chicharrón), pickled radish, chive oil

TECHNIQUE

  1. Blend the cold veal and fat with ice and seasonings into a smooth farce. Fill the casings and twist into links. Poach at 165 °F for 20 minutes, ice, then sear in butter to order.
  2. Shape small brioche knots, dip in the soda bath, salt and bake at 400 °F for 12 minutes.
  3. Whisk the mustards with the reduced beer, then the oil, into a glossy emulsion.
  4. Blend the braised sauerkraut with butter into a smooth, tangy purée.
A golden sausage on a swoosh of sauerkraut purée, dots of beer-mustard emulsion, shards of crisp pork skin, pickled radish and chive oil, with a warm pretzel brioche on a small board beside it.
