Foie Gras

Rustic version ↓ Michelin version ↓

The story behind the dish

The ancient Egyptians already noticed that migrating geese gorged themselves before long flights, and the Romans fattened geese on figs. In France, foie gras became the pride of the southwest, Gascony and Périgord, and in 2006 French law declared it part of the country’s protected cultural and gastronomic heritage.

It is served at Christmas and New Year, either cold as a terrine with toasted brioche and a sweet wine like Sauternes, or seared hot with fruit. Its production remains debated, and some countries and cities have banned it.

THE RUSTIC VERSION

Terrine for the Holidays

Serves 8–10 · 1½ hours, plus 3 days resting
A simple home terrine of duck foie gras, seasoned, baked gently and rested for several days.

INGREDIENTS

  • 1 whole raw duck foie gras (about 1¼ lb), grade A, at room temperature
  • 1½ tsp fine sea salt
  • ½ tsp ground white pepper
  • ¼ tsp sugar
  • 2 tbsp Sauternes, Armagnac or Cognac
  • To serve: toasted brioche or country bread, fig jam or onion confit, flaky salt

METHOD

  1. Let the liver soften for 1 hour. Separate the two lobes and gently open each one; with the tip of a small knife and your fingers, pull out the main veins.
  2. Season both sides with the salt, pepper and sugar and sprinkle with the wine or spirit.
  3. Press the lobes into a small terrine dish (about 1-quart), smooth side out, packing tightly. Cover and refrigerate for 12 hours.
  4. Heat the oven to 200 °F. Set the terrine in a water bath of 160 °F water and bake for about 35–40 minutes, until the center reaches 125 °F.
  5. Pour off the excess fat (keep it), place a piece of cardboard cut to size on top and weigh down lightly. Chill overnight, then spoon a little of the reserved fat over the top to seal.
  6. Refrigerate for 2–3 days before serving in slices with toasted brioche and jam.
THE MICHELIN 3-STAR VERSION

Foie Gras, Fig and Sauternes

Serves 4 · 1½ hours
Seared duck foie gras with roasted figs, a Sauternes and black pepper gel, toasted brioche and walnut crumble.

INGREDIENTS

  • 4 slices (3 oz each) raw grade A duck foie gras, ¾ inch thick, chilled
  • 6 fresh figs, halved
  • 1 tbsp honey and 1 sprig thyme
  • Sauternes gel: 1 cup Sauternes, 2 tbsp sugar, ½ tsp agar-agar, cracked black pepper
  • 4 thick slices brioche, cut into neat rectangles
  • Walnut crumble: ¼ cup walnuts, 2 tbsp butter, 2 tbsp flour, 1 tbsp brown sugar
  • Fleur de sel, aged balsamic, micro herbs

TECHNIQUE

  1. Sauternes gel: boil the wine with the sugar and agar for 1 minute, set in the fridge, then blend until smooth. Add a little cracked pepper.
  2. Walnut crumble: rub the ingredients together, bake at 340 °F for 12 minutes, cool and crumble.
  3. Roast the figs cut side up with the honey and thyme at 400 °F for 8 minutes.
  4. Toast the brioche in a dry pan until golden on all sides.
  5. Score the foie gras lightly, season, and sear in a very hot dry pan for 45–60 seconds per side, until deeply browned and soft inside. Rest 1 minute on paper towel.
The brioche rectangle, the seared foie gras resting on it, three roasted fig halves alongside, dots of Sauternes gel, a line of walnut crumble, a few drops of aged balsamic and fleur de sel on the foie gras.
