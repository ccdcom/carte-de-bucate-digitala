Swiss Chocolate

The Art of Swiss Chocolate
Rustic version ↓ Michelin version ↓

The story behind the dish

Switzerland turned chocolate into a craft. In 1875 Daniel Peter invented milk chocolate in Vevey, using the condensed milk of his neighbor Henri Nestlé. In 1879 Rodolphe Lindt invented conching, long hours of stirring that made chocolate melt on the tongue.

Swiss chocolate became a byword for smoothness and balance, and the truffle, a ball of ganache rolled in cocoa, is one of the simplest ways to enjoy it at home.

THE RUSTIC VERSION

Chocolate Truffles at Home

Makes about 30 · 30 minutes, plus 3 hours chilling
Dark ganache rolled by hand in cocoa: the easiest way to bring Swiss chocolate home.

INGREDIENTS

  • 8 oz good Swiss dark chocolate (60–70%), finely chopped
  • ⅔ cup heavy cream
  • 2 tbsp butter, soft
  • 1 tbsp honey
  • 1 tbsp kirsch, Cognac or orange liqueur (optional)
  • Unsweetened cocoa powder for rolling

METHOD

  1. Bring the cream and honey to a simmer and pour over the chocolate. Leave 2 minutes, then stir from the center until smooth.
  2. Stir in the butter and liqueur.
  3. Cover and chill for 2–3 hours until firm enough to scoop.
  4. Scoop small balls with a teaspoon or melon baller and roll quickly between cool hands.
  5. Roll in cocoa powder and keep in the fridge. Serve slightly below room temperature.
THE MICHELIN 3-STAR VERSION

Grand Cru, Milk and Alps

Serves 6 · 1 day
A dark chocolate study: Grand Cru crémeux, milk chocolate mousse, cocoa nib tuile, hay ice cream and a warm chocolate sauce.

INGREDIENTS

  • Crémeux: 1 cup cream, ½ cup milk, 3 yolks, 2 tbsp sugar, 5 oz Swiss Grand Cru dark chocolate (70%)
  • Milk chocolate mousse: 4 oz Swiss milk chocolate, 1 cup heavy cream whipped, 1 yolk
  • Hay ice cream: 2 cups milk and cream infused with a handful of clean, toasted hay, 4 yolks, ½ cup sugar
  • Cocoa nib tuile: 2 tbsp butter, 3 tbsp sugar, 1 tbsp glucose, 3 tbsp cocoa nibs
  • Warm sauce: 3 oz dark chocolate, ⅓ cup milk
  • Fleur de sel, cocoa powder

TECHNIQUE

  1. Crémeux: make a custard to 180 °F, pour over the chocolate, blend and chill overnight.
  2. Mousse: fold the melted chocolate and yolk into the whipped cream; chill.
  3. Ice cream: toast the hay in a dry pan, infuse in the hot milk and cream for 30 minutes, strain, make a custard, chill and churn.
  4. Tuile: melt the butter, sugar and glucose, add the nibs, spread thin and bake at 340 °F for 8 minutes.
  5. Warm the sauce just before serving.
A swoosh of crémeux, a quenelle of milk chocolate mousse, a quenelle of hay ice cream, a shard of nib tuile, a few flakes of salt, a dusting of cocoa and warm chocolate sauce poured at the table.
