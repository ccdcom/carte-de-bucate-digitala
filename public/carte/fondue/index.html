🇨🇭 Switzerland

Fondue

Rustic version ↓ Michelin version ↓

The story behind the dish

Cheese melted with wine was a way for Alpine families to use hard cheese and stale bread in winter. A recipe appears in Zürich in 1699, but fondue became the national dish only in the 1930s, when the Swiss Cheese Union promoted it to sell more cheese, with the slogan “fondue creates a good mood.”

The classic moitié-moitié is half Gruyère and half Vacherin Fribourgeois. The rules are serious: whoever drops their bread in the pot pays the next round, and the golden crust left at the bottom, la religieuse, is the best part.

THE RUSTIC VERSION

Moitié-Moitié by the Fire

Serves 4 · 25 minutes
Half Gruyère, half Vacherin, white wine and kirsch, with cubes of bread.

INGREDIENTS

  • 1 garlic clove, halved
  • 1¼ cups dry white wine (Fendant/Chasselas)
  • 14 oz Gruyère, coarsely grated
  • 14 oz Vacherin Fribourgeois (or Emmental), grated
  • 1 tbsp cornstarch dissolved in 3 tbsp kirsch
  • Nutmeg and black pepper
  • 1 large day-old baguette or country loaf, in 1-inch cubes with crust
  • Small potatoes, pickles and pearl onions (optional)

METHOD

  1. Rub the inside of a caquelon (fondue pot) or heavy saucepan with the garlic.
  2. Heat the wine until steaming. Add the cheese a handful at a time, stirring in a figure-eight with a wooden spoon, over medium-low heat.
  3. When the cheese has melted, stir in the cornstarch and kirsch and keep stirring until creamy and just bubbling.
  4. Season with nutmeg and pepper.
  5. Set the pot over a burner at the table, keeping it at a low simmer. Spear bread cubes on long forks, dip and stir.
  6. Let a crust form at the bottom at the end, and share it.
Tip. Drink white wine or black tea with fondue, never cold water: the Swiss swear it helps digestion.
THE MICHELIN 3-STAR VERSION

Fondue, Truffled and Aerated

Serves 4 as a starter · 1 hour
A light fondue of aged Gruyère and Vacherin served as a warm espuma, with crisp bread, pickled vegetables, potato and shaved black truffle.

INGREDIENTS

  • 7 oz Gruyère d’alpage (aged 18 months) and 7 oz Vacherin Fribourgeois, grated
  • 1 cup Chasselas, 1 garlic clove, 1 tbsp kirsch, 1 tsp cornstarch
  • ⅓ cup heavy cream
  • A siphon with 2 N₂O cartridges
  • Sourdough, cut into thin batons and toasted
  • Fingerling potatoes, roasted; pickled pearl onions and cornichons
  • 1 small black winter truffle (optional), or truffle oil

TECHNIQUE

  1. Make a smooth fondue with the wine, garlic, cheeses, kirsch and cornstarch.
  2. Blend in the cream, strain through a fine sieve and pour into a siphon. Charge and keep warm in a 140 °F water bath.
  3. Toast the bread batons until crisp.
  4. Roast the potatoes until golden.
A warm bowl filled with a cloud of fondue espuma, shaved truffle on top, and around it on a wooden board: bread batons, roasted potatoes, pickled onions and cornichons for dipping.
