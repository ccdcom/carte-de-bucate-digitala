🇵🇱 Poland

Karpatka

Carpathian Cream Cake
Rustic version ↓ Michelin version ↓

The story behind the dish

Karpatka is younger than it looks: it entered Polish home kitchens in the 1950s and became one of the country’s most loved cakes. Two layers of choux pastry, baked rough and uneven like the peaks of the Carpathian mountains, hold a thick vanilla cream.

Dusted with powdered sugar like fresh snow, it is in effect a giant éclair: crisp at the edges, soft inside, and best eaten with a coffee in the afternoon.

THE RUSTIC VERSION

The Polish Kitchen Karpatka

Serves 12 · 2 hours, plus chilling
Two rugged choux layers and a rich vanilla custard-butter cream.

INGREDIENTS

  • Choux: 1 cup water, ½ cup butter, pinch of salt, 1¼ cups all-purpose flour, 5 eggs
  • Cream: 3 cups milk, ¾ cup sugar, 2 egg yolks, ⅓ cup cornstarch, 2 tbsp flour, 2 tsp vanilla, 1 cup butter, soft
  • Powdered sugar

METHOD

  1. Heat the oven to 400 °F. Line two 9 × 13-inch pans (or one, baking twice) with parchment.
  2. Bring the water, butter and salt to a boil. Add the flour all at once and stir hard over the heat for 2 minutes, until the dough forms a ball.
  3. Cool 5 minutes, then beat in the eggs one at a time until glossy.
  4. Spread half the dough thinly and roughly in each pan; it should look uneven. Bake for 25–30 minutes, until puffed and golden. Do not open the oven in the first 20 minutes. Cool.
  5. Cream: heat 2 cups of milk with the sugar. Whisk the rest of the milk with the yolks, cornstarch, flour and vanilla, pour into the hot milk and cook, whisking, until very thick. Cover the surface and cool completely.
  6. Beat the butter until fluffy and add the custard a spoonful at a time.
  7. Spread the cream on the first choux layer, top with the second, rough side up. Chill for 3 hours and dust heavily with powdered sugar.
THE MICHELIN 3-STAR VERSION

Karpatka, Peaks and Snow

Serves 6 · 4 hours
Crisp craquelin choux ‘mountains’, a light vanilla diplomat cream, rhubarb compote and a sour cream snow.

INGREDIENTS

  • Choux: as in the classic, piped into small irregular peaks
  • Craquelin: 3 tbsp butter, ¼ cup brown sugar, ¼ cup flour, rolled thin and cut in rounds
  • Diplomat cream: 1 cup milk, 1 vanilla bean, 3 yolks, ¼ cup sugar, 2 tbsp cornstarch, folded with ¾ cup whipped cream
  • Rhubarb: 2 stalks, poached in syrup with a strip of orange peel
  • Sour cream snow: ½ cup sour cream mixed with 2 tbsp powdered sugar, frozen and grated (or liquid nitrogen powder)
  • Powdered sugar, crushed freeze-dried raspberries

TECHNIQUE

  1. Pipe small rough peaks of choux, top each with a craquelin round and bake at 375 °F for 25 minutes until crisp.
  2. Make the vanilla pastry cream, chill, then fold in the whipped cream.
  3. Poach the rhubarb until just tender; reduce the syrup.
  4. Fill the choux peaks from below with diplomat cream.
A spoon of rhubarb compote, three filled choux peaks of different heights like a mountain range, sour cream snow grated over, a heavy dusting of powdered sugar and a few crumbs of raspberry.
