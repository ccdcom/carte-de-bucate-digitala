Moussaka

Eggplant and Meat Casserole
Rustic version ↓ Michelin version ↓

The story behind the dish

Versions of moussaka exist across the Middle East and the Balkans, including Romania, but the layered Greek casserole known today was shaped in the 1920s by chef Nikolaos Tselementes, who crowned it with a thick French-style béchamel.

Fried eggplant, a cinnamon-scented meat sauce and that golden, custardy topping: it is the Sunday dish of Greek families, always better when allowed to rest before cutting.

THE RUSTIC VERSION

The Sunday Tray

Serves 8 · 2½ hours
Eggplant, spiced meat sauce and béchamel, baked in a deep tray for the whole family.

INGREDIENTS

  • 3 large eggplants, in ½-inch slices
  • Olive oil and salt
  • Meat sauce: 2 lb ground beef or lamb, 1 large onion, 2 garlic cloves, ½ cup red wine, 1 can (14 oz) crushed tomatoes, 1 tbsp tomato paste, 1 cinnamon stick, 3 allspice berries, 1 bay leaf, parsley
  • Béchamel: ½ cup butter, ¾ cup flour, 4 cups warm milk, 2 egg yolks, ½ cup grated kefalotyri or Parmesan, nutmeg
  • 2 potatoes, thinly sliced (optional)

METHOD

  1. Salt the eggplant slices and leave for 30 minutes. Pat dry, brush with olive oil and roast at 400 °F for 20 minutes until soft and golden. (If using potatoes, roast them the same way.)
  2. Meat sauce: soften the onion and garlic in olive oil, brown the meat, add the wine and let it evaporate. Add the tomatoes, paste and spices and simmer for 40 minutes until thick. Remove the whole spices and stir in parsley.
  3. Béchamel: melt the butter, stir in the flour for 2 minutes, then whisk in the milk and cook until thick. Off the heat, whisk in the yolks, half the cheese and a pinch of nutmeg.
  4. In a 9 × 13-inch dish, layer potatoes (if using), half the eggplant, all the meat sauce and the rest of the eggplant.
  5. Pour over the béchamel, smooth it and sprinkle with the remaining cheese.
  6. Bake at 350 °F for 45–50 minutes, until deep golden. Rest at least 30 minutes before cutting.
Tip. Moussaka cuts best when it has cooled for an hour; it is just as good warm as hot.
THE MICHELIN 3-STAR VERSION

Moussaka, Lamb and Smoke

Serves 4 · 5 hours
Braised lamb neck, smoked eggplant purée, eggplant ‘sheets’ and a light kefalotyri béchamel gratinated to order.

INGREDIENTS

  • 1½ lb lamb neck, on the bone
  • 1 onion, 1 carrot, 3 garlic cloves, 1 cinnamon stick, 2 allspice, ½ cup red wine, 1 cup tomato passata, 2 cups lamb stock
  • 3 eggplants: 2 charred whole over a flame for purée, 1 sliced paper-thin lengthwise
  • Light béchamel: 2 tbsp butter, 2 tbsp flour, 1 cup milk, 1 egg yolk, ⅓ cup grated kefalotyri, nutmeg
  • Tomato confit: 12 cherry tomatoes, olive oil, thyme
  • Oregano, mint, crisp shallots

TECHNIQUE

  1. Brown the lamb, add the vegetables, spices, wine, passata and stock, cover and braise at 300 °F for 3 hours. Pick the meat into strands, reduce the liquid to a glaze and fold half into the meat.
  2. Scoop the charred eggplant flesh, blend with olive oil, lemon and salt to a smoky purée.
  3. Brush the eggplant slices with oil and bake flat between parchment at 325 °F for 15 minutes, until pliable and golden.
  4. Confit the tomatoes in olive oil with thyme at 250 °F for 1 hour.
  5. Make the béchamel, finishing with the yolk and cheese.
  6. Press the lamb into 3-inch rings, wrap with eggplant sheets, top with béchamel and brown under a very hot broiler.
