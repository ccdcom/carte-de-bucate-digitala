Gazpacho

Andalusian Cold Soup
Rustic version ↓ Michelin version ↓

The story behind the dish

Long before tomatoes reached Spain, Andalusian field workers pounded stale bread with garlic, olive oil, vinegar and water: a cold, sustaining soup for the hottest days. When tomatoes and peppers arrived from the Americas, they turned it red and gave us the gazpacho we know.

Today it is blended rather than pounded, served in glasses or bowls, and kept in every Spanish fridge from June to September. Its cousin salmorejo, from Córdoba, is thicker and topped with egg and ham.

THE RUSTIC VERSION

Andalusian Summer Gazpacho

Serves 6 · 20 minutes, plus 2 hours chilling
Ripe vegetables, bread, olive oil and sherry vinegar, blended and chilled.

INGREDIENTS

  • 2½ lb very ripe tomatoes, roughly chopped
  • 1 cucumber, peeled and chopped
  • 1 green Italian or bell pepper, seeded
  • 1 small garlic clove
  • ¼ small onion (optional)
  • 2 oz day-old bread, crusts removed, soaked in water
  • ½ cup extra-virgin olive oil
  • 2–3 tbsp sherry vinegar
  • 1½ tsp salt
  • To serve: diced cucumber, pepper, onion and croutons

METHOD

  1. Put the tomatoes, cucumber, pepper, garlic, onion and squeezed bread in a blender with the salt and vinegar.
  2. Blend until very smooth.
  3. With the motor running, pour in the olive oil in a thin stream until the soup turns creamy and orange-pink.
  4. Pass through a sieve for a silkier texture. Taste and adjust salt and vinegar.
  5. Chill for at least 2 hours.
  6. Serve very cold in glasses or bowls, with the diced vegetables and croutons on the side.
THE MICHELIN 3-STAR VERSION

Gazpacho, Red and Clear

Serves 4 · 1 day
A velvety red gazpacho poured at the table over tomato tartare, cucumber pearls, olive oil ice cream and crisp bread.

INGREDIENTS

  • Gazpacho: as for the classic version, made with the ripest heirloom tomatoes and Picual olive oil
  • Tomato tartare: 2 ripe tomatoes, peeled, seeded and finely diced, 1 tsp chopped shallot, chives, sherry vinegar
  • Cucumber pearls: 1 cucumber, scooped with a tiny melon baller
  • Olive oil ice cream: 1 cup whole milk, ½ cup cream, 3 yolks, ⅓ cup sugar, ½ cup fruity olive oil, pinch of salt
  • Bread: thin slices of baguette, brushed with oil and baked
  • Basil and pepper cress, flaky salt

TECHNIQUE

  1. Make the gazpacho the day before, pass it twice through a fine sieve and chill well.
  2. Ice cream: make a custard with the milk, cream, yolks and sugar to 180 °F, cool to lukewarm, blend in the olive oil and salt, chill and churn.
  3. Season the tomato tartare with shallot, chives, salt and a few drops of vinegar.
  4. Bake the bread slices at 325 °F until crisp and golden.
In a cold shallow bowl: a small ring of tomato tartare, cucumber pearls around it, a quenelle of olive oil ice cream on top, a shard of crisp bread and a few herbs. At the table, pour the cold gazpacho around and finish with a thread of olive oil.
