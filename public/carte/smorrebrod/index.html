🇩🇰 Denmark

Smørrebrød

Danish Open-Faced Sandwich
Rustic version ↓ Michelin version ↓

The story behind the dish

Smørrebrød, “butter and bread,” began in the 1800s as the lunch of Danish farm and factory workers: dense rye bread (rugbrød) topped with herring, leftovers or cold cuts. In Copenhagen it became an art, with restaurants like Ida Davidsen offering menus of hundreds of combinations.

There are rules: the bread is always buttered, toppings are arranged with care, herring comes first, then fish, then meat, then cheese. It is eaten with a knife and fork, and often with a cold beer and a snaps.

THE RUSTIC VERSION

A Copenhagen Lunch Table

Serves 4 (12 pieces) · 45 minutes
Three classics on buttered rye: pickled herring, egg and shrimp, and roast pork with red cabbage.

INGREDIENTS

  • 12 thin slices of Danish rye bread (rugbrød), buttered generously
  • Herring: 8 pieces of pickled herring, red onion rings, capers, dill
  • Egg and shrimp: 4 hard-boiled eggs, sliced, 6 oz cooked small shrimp, mayonnaise, lemon, dill
  • Pork: 8 oz cold roast pork, sliced, ½ cup pickled red cabbage, 4 pieces of crackling, sliced cucumber pickles
  • Remoulade (Danish relish mayonnaise), chives, cress

METHOD

  1. Butter the rye bread all the way to the edges; the butter keeps the bread from going soggy.
  2. Herring: lay two pieces of herring on each of 4 slices, top with onion rings, capers and dill.
  3. Egg and shrimp: cover 4 slices with egg slices, pile shrimp on top, add a little mayonnaise, a lemon twist and dill.
  4. Pork: fold the pork slices on 4 slices, add red cabbage, a cucumber pickle and crackling.
  5. Arrange on a board and eat in order: herring, then shrimp, then pork.
THE MICHELIN 3-STAR VERSION

Smørrebrød, New Nordic

Serves 4 as a starter · 2 hours
Three precise open sandwiches on toasted sourdough rye: cured mackerel, hand-cut beef tartare, and new potato with smoked cheese.

INGREDIENTS

  • 12 small rectangles of sourdough rye, toasted in brown butter
  • Mackerel: 2 fillets cured in salt and sugar for 1 hour, lightly torched; pickled cucumber, horseradish cream, dill
  • Tartare: 6 oz beef tenderloin, hand-chopped, capers, shallot, egg yolk emulsion, crisp potato, pickled onion petals
  • Potato: small new potatoes, sliced, smoked cheese cream (rygeost or smoked ricotta), radish, chives, crisp chicken skin crumbs
  • Cress, edible flowers, flaky salt

TECHNIQUE

  1. Cure the mackerel, rinse, dry and torch the skin until blistered. Cut neat pieces.
  2. Season the tartare with capers and shallot; keep cold.
  3. Cook the new potatoes, cool and slice.
  4. Toast the rye in brown butter until crisp at the edges.
On a long slate: the mackerel smørrebrød with cucumber and horseradish cream, the tartare with yolk emulsion and crisp potato, the potato with smoked cheese cream, radish and chicken skin crumbs. Each finished with cress, flowers and flaky salt.
