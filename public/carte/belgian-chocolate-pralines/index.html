🇧🇪 Belgium

Belgian Chocolate Pralines

Rustic version ↓ Michelin version ↓

The story behind the dish

In 1912 Jean Neuhaus, a Brussels pharmacist turned confectioner, invented the filled chocolate he called a praline: a hard chocolate shell around a soft center. Three years later his wife, Louise Agostini, designed the ballotin, the elegant cardboard box that still holds them.

Belgian pralines are filled with ganache, nut pralinés, creams and caramels. Brussels’ Grand-Place and Sablon are lined with chocolate shops, and a ballotin is the classic gift for any visit.

THE RUSTIC VERSION

Simple Home Pralines

Makes about 24 · 2 hours, plus setting
Chocolate shells filled with a hazelnut gianduja and a dark ganache, made with a silicone mold.

INGREDIENTS

  • 12 oz good dark chocolate (Belgian, 55–70%) for the shells
  • Ganache: 3 oz dark chocolate, ⅓ cup heavy cream, 1 tsp butter
  • Hazelnut filling: 3 oz milk chocolate melted with ½ cup hazelnut paste or Nutella-style spread
  • A silicone or polycarbonate praline mold

METHOD

  1. Melt two-thirds of the shell chocolate gently over a water bath. Remove from the heat and stir in the remaining chopped chocolate until smooth and slightly cool, for a glossy finish.
  2. Fill the mold cavities, tap to release bubbles, then turn the mold upside down over a bowl to let the excess run out, leaving thin shells. Scrape clean and chill for 10 minutes.
  3. Ganache: pour hot cream over the chopped chocolate, stir until smooth and add the butter. Cool until thick but still pourable.
  4. Mix the melted milk chocolate with the hazelnut paste.
  5. Pipe the fillings into the shells, leaving about ⅛ inch at the top. Chill for 30 minutes.
  6. Seal with the remaining melted chocolate, scrape flat and chill until set. Turn out gently.
THE MICHELIN 3-STAR VERSION

A Ballotin of Four

Makes 32 · 2 days
Four tempered pralines: raspberry and dark ganache, roasted hazelnut praliné with feuilletine, salted caramel, and speculoos and coffee.

INGREDIENTS

  • 1¼ lb Belgian dark couverture (70%) for tempering, plus cocoa butter colors (optional)
  • Raspberry ganache: ½ cup raspberry purée, 3 oz dark chocolate, 1 tbsp butter, 1 tsp glucose
  • Hazelnut praliné: 1 cup roasted hazelnuts, ½ cup sugar, 2 oz milk chocolate, ½ cup feuilletine
  • Salted caramel: ½ cup sugar, ⅓ cup cream, 2 tbsp butter, pinch of fleur de sel
  • Speculoos coffee ganache: 3 oz white chocolate, ¼ cup cream infused with coffee beans, 2 tbsp speculoos spread
  • Polycarbonate molds

TECHNIQUE

  1. Polish the molds with cotton. Brush or spray with colored cocoa butter for a jewel-like finish, if using.
  2. Temper the couverture: melt to 122 °F, cool to 82 °F while stirring, then rewarm to 88–90 °F.
  3. Make thin shells with the tempered chocolate and let them crystallize.
  4. Make the four fillings and let each cool to about 82 °F before piping into the shells.
  5. Crystallize the fillings overnight at cool room temperature, then seal with tempered chocolate.
  6. Release from the molds when fully set, after a few hours in a cool place.
Eight pralines, two of each, set in a small ballotin or on a dark slate, glossy and colored, served with an espresso at the end of the meal.
