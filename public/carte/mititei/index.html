Grilled Skinless Sausages
Rustic version ↓ Michelin version ↓

The story behind the dish

Legend places their birth in a Bucharest tavern in the late 1800s, when a cook ran out of sausage casings and threw the seasoned meat straight on the grill. True or not, the skinless little sausage became the taste of Romanian summer, of the 1st of May and of every garden barbecue.

The secret is a garlicky, thyme-scented mix of beef and pork, loosened with broth and a pinch of baking soda that makes them springy and juicy. They are eaten hot from the grill with mustard, fresh bread and a cold beer.

THE RUSTIC VERSION

Garden Grill Mici

Makes about 24 (serves 6) · 30 minutes, plus overnight resting
The classic Bucharest recipe, made the day before so the flavors settle.

INGREDIENTS

  • 1½ lb ground beef chuck (about 20% fat)
  • 1 lb ground pork shoulder
  • 6 garlic cloves, crushed to a paste
  • 2 tsp salt
  • 1½ tsp freshly ground black pepper
  • 1 tsp dried thyme, 1 tsp sweet paprika, ½ tsp ground caraway, ½ tsp ground allspice
  • 1 tsp baking soda
  • 1 cup cold beef or bone broth
  • Juice of ½ lemon
  • To serve: yellow mustard, fresh bread, pickled peppers

METHOD

  1. Put both meats in a large bowl with the garlic, salt and all the spices.
  2. Dissolve the baking soda in the lemon juice (it will fizz) and add it to the meat.
  3. Knead with your hands, adding the cold broth a little at a time, for about 10 minutes, until the mixture is very sticky and soft but still holds its shape.
  4. Cover and refrigerate overnight, or for at least 6 hours.
  5. With wet hands, shape into cylinders about 3½ inches long and ¾ inch thick.
  6. Grill over medium-hot charcoal for 8–10 minutes, turning often, until browned outside and juicy inside.
  7. Serve at once with plenty of mustard, bread and pickles.
Tip. Oil the grill grate well and do not move the mici for the first 2 minutes, so they release cleanly.
THE MICHELIN 3-STAR VERSION

Mici, Ember and Smoke

Serves 4 · 2 hours, plus overnight resting
Hand-chopped heritage meat cooked over binchotan, with a mustard-seed caviar, smoked pepper purée and a crisp of bread.

INGREDIENTS

  • 10 oz dry-aged beef chuck and 6 oz Mangalica pork shoulder, hand-chopped very finely
  • 2 oz pork back fat, frozen and finely diced
  • 3 roasted garlic cloves and 1 raw garlic clove, crushed
  • 1 tsp salt, 1 tsp toasted and ground black pepper, ½ tsp each thyme, caraway and allspice
  • ½ tsp baking soda and ⅓ cup reduced beef consommé, chilled
  • Mustard caviar: 3 tbsp yellow mustard seeds, ⅓ cup white wine vinegar, 2 tbsp sugar, ⅓ cup water
  • Pepper purée: 2 red bell peppers, charred, 1 tbsp olive oil, smoked salt
  • Bread crisp: 4 paper-thin slices of sourdough, brushed with lard
  • To finish: lovage or celery leaves, pickled shallot rings

TECHNIQUE

  1. Combine the meats, back fat, garlic and spices. Add the baking soda dissolved in a spoonful of consommé, then work in the rest of the consommé until tacky. Rest overnight in the fridge.
  2. Mustard caviar: simmer the seeds with the vinegar, sugar and water for 30–40 minutes, until plump and glossy, adding water if needed. Cool.
  3. Pepper purée: peel and seed the charred peppers, blend with the oil until silky, season with smoked salt and pass through a fine sieve.
  4. Bake the bread slices between two sheets of parchment at 325 °F for 12 minutes, until golden and brittle.
  5. Shape the meat into small, even cylinders of about 1½ oz each.
  6. Grill over very hot charcoal or binchotan for 6–7 minutes, turning often, until charred at the edges and pink-juicy at the core. Rest 2 minutes.
