🇳🇱 Netherlands

Stamppot

with Smoked Sausage
Rustic version ↓ Michelin version ↓

The story behind the dish

Stamppot, literally “mash pot,” is the winter food of Dutch farmers and laborers: potatoes and a vegetable boiled together and mashed. Kale gives boerenkool, carrots and onions give hutspot, sauerkraut gives zuurkool. Hutspot is even linked to the 1574 siege of Leiden, when the starving city was relieved and soldiers found a pot of it in the abandoned Spanish camp.

Crowned with a rookworst, the Dutch smoked sausage, and a pool of gravy in a hollow of the mash (the kuiltje), it is pure comfort after a cold day of cycling or skating.

THE RUSTIC VERSION

Boerenkool for a Cold Night

Serves 4 · 45 minutes
Potatoes and kale mashed together, smoked sausage and a pool of gravy.

INGREDIENTS

  • 2½ lb floury potatoes, peeled and quartered
  • 1½ lb curly kale, finely shredded (or a bag of pre-cut kale)
  • 1 large smoked sausage (rookworst, about 1 lb) or Polish kielbasa
  • 4 oz smoked bacon, in lardons
  • 3 tbsp butter
  • ½ cup warm milk
  • Salt, pepper, a little nutmeg
  • Mustard and gravy (jus) to serve; pickled gherkins and onions

METHOD

  1. Put the potatoes in a large pot, cover with salted water and lay the kale on top. Cover and cook for 20 minutes, until the potatoes are soft.
  2. Heat the sausage in hot (not boiling) water for 20 minutes.
  3. Fry the bacon until crisp.
  4. Drain the potatoes and kale and mash them roughly together with the butter and warm milk. Season with salt, pepper and nutmeg, and stir in the bacon.
  5. Heap the stamppot on plates, make a hollow in the center with a spoon and fill it with gravy.
  6. Slice the sausage and lay it on top. Serve with mustard and pickles.
THE MICHELIN 3-STAR VERSION

Stamppot, Smoke and Silk

Serves 4 · 1 day
Smoked pork belly, an ultra-silky kale mash, crisp kale, smoked sausage crumb and a mustard-beer jus.

INGREDIENTS

  • 1½ lb pork belly, brined overnight, cold-smoked lightly and slow-cooked
  • 1 lb Yukon Gold potatoes and ½ cup butter, for a silky mash
  • 8 oz cavolo nero or curly kale: half blanched and blended into purée, half baked into crisps
  • 4 oz rookworst, diced small and fried crisp
  • Jus: 1 cup pork jus, ½ cup Dutch amber beer, 1 tbsp whole-grain mustard, 1 tbsp butter
  • Pickled pearl onions, fried capers

TECHNIQUE

  1. Cook the brined belly in a covered dish at 250 °F for 6 hours. Press under a weight overnight, then cut into neat blocks.
  2. Make a very smooth potato purée, then fold in the kale purée and a little shredded blanched kale.
  3. Bake kale leaves brushed with oil at 300 °F until crisp.
  4. Reduce the jus with the beer, then whisk in the mustard and butter.
  5. Crisp the belly blocks skin side down in a hot pan until the crackling puffs.
