Meringues with Gruyère Double Cream

Rustic version ↓ Michelin version ↓

The story behind the dish

In the Gruyère region of Fribourg, dairy farmers had more cream than they could use. Crisp meringues became its perfect partner, and by the late 19th century meringues with crème double de la Gruyère were the Sunday treat of Alpine villages.

The double cream is so thick it stands on the spoon, with a fat content around 50%. Little else is needed: some add berries, some a drizzle of chocolate, but purists keep it white on white.

THE RUSTIC VERSION

The Mountain Inn Dessert

Serves 6 · 3 hours (mostly drying)
Crisp meringues and a mountain of thick cream, with summer berries.

INGREDIENTS

  • 4 egg whites, at room temperature
  • 1 cup superfine sugar
  • Pinch of salt
  • 1½ cups double cream or the thickest heavy cream you can find (or heavy cream mixed with ¼ cup mascarpone)
  • Fresh raspberries or strawberries (optional)

METHOD

  1. Heat the oven to 210 °F and line two baking sheets.
  2. Beat the whites with the salt to soft peaks. Add the sugar a spoonful at a time and keep beating until stiff, glossy and the sugar has dissolved.
  3. Pipe or spoon 12 large meringues onto the sheets.
  4. Bake for 2–2½ hours, until crisp and dry, then leave in the turned-off oven to cool.
  5. Whip the cream lightly (or not at all if it is real double cream).
  6. Serve two meringues per person with a generous heap of cream and berries.
THE MICHELIN 3-STAR VERSION

Gruyère Cream, Meringue and Alpine Flowers

Serves 6 · 1 day
Glass-like meringue shards, double cream ice cream, Gruyère cream, wild strawberries and an elderflower jelly.

INGREDIENTS

  • Meringue: 3 egg whites, ¾ cup sugar, vanilla seeds
  • Double cream ice cream: 2 cups double cream, 1 cup milk, ½ cup sugar, 4 yolks
  • Whipped double cream with a pinch of salt and honey
  • Elderflower jelly: ½ cup elderflower syrup, ½ cup water, ½ tsp agar-agar
  • Wild strawberries (or small strawberries), alpine flowers and herbs (mint, lemon thyme)

TECHNIQUE

  1. Spread the meringue paper-thin on a silicone mat and dry at 175 °F for 3 hours. Break into shards.
  2. Make the ice cream: custard to 180 °F, chill, churn.
  3. Set the elderflower jelly and cut into small cubes.
  4. Whip the double cream with honey and salt until it just holds.
