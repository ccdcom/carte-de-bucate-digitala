Pasta alla Carbonara

Classic Roman Pasta
Rustic version ↓ Michelin version ↓

The story behind the dish

Carbonara is younger than most people think. No written recipe appears before the 1940s, when Roman cooks combined pasta with eggs and cured pork in the hungry years around the end of the war. By the 1950s it belonged to every trattoria in Rome.

Its whole secret is technique: guanciale, egg, Pecorino Romano and black pepper, brought together with starchy pasta water into a sauce that is silky, never scrambled. In Rome there is no cream, no garlic and no onion, and Romans will tell you so.

THE RUSTIC VERSION

The Roman Trattoria Way

Serves 4 · 25 minutes
Five ingredients and ten minutes of attention, the way it is made in Testaccio.

INGREDIENTS

  • 14 oz spaghetti or rigatoni
  • 5 oz guanciale (cured pork jowl), cut into thick strips; pancetta will do
  • 4 egg yolks and 1 whole egg, at room temperature
  • ¾ cup finely grated Pecorino Romano, plus extra to serve
  • Plenty of freshly ground black pepper
  • Salt for the pasta water (go lightly: the cheese and pork are salty)

METHOD

  1. Bring a large pot of water to a boil and salt it lightly.
  2. Put the guanciale in a large cold skillet and cook over medium-low heat for 8–10 minutes, until the fat has melted and the edges are crisp. Turn off the heat and keep the fat in the pan.
  3. In a bowl, whisk the yolks, the whole egg, the Pecorino and a generous amount of black pepper into a thick paste.
  4. Cook the pasta for 1 minute less than the package says. Scoop out a mug of the cooking water before draining.
  5. Tip the pasta into the pan with the guanciale, off the heat, and toss for 30 seconds so every strand is coated in fat. Let it stand for 1 minute to cool slightly.
  6. Whisk 2 tablespoons of hot pasta water into the egg mixture, pour it over the pasta and toss quickly and constantly, adding splashes of water until the sauce is creamy and glossy. If it is too runny, return the pan to very low heat for a few seconds while tossing; the eggs must thicken, not scramble.
  7. Serve at once with more Pecorino and black pepper.
Tip. The pan must be off the heat when the eggs go in. Carbonara waits for no one, so have the plates ready.
THE MICHELIN 3-STAR VERSION

Carbonara, Yolk and Glass

Serves 4 · 1 hour
A nest of fresh tonnarelli in a pecorino sabayon, crowned with a raw yolk the guest breaks at the table and a shard of glass-crisp guanciale.

INGREDIENTS

  • 11 oz fresh egg tonnarelli or tagliolini (or a top-quality dried spaghettoni)
  • 5 oz guanciale: 8 paper-thin slices, the rest finely diced
  • 8 very fresh free-range eggs: 4 yolks for the sabayon, 4 yolks to crown the plates
  • ⅔ cup finely grated Pecorino Romano DOP, plus a piece kept in the freezer
  • 1 tbsp Tellicherry black peppercorns
  • Flaky sea salt

TECHNIQUE

  1. Lay the guanciale slices between two sheets of parchment, press flat between two baking sheets and bake at 325 °F for 15–18 minutes, until glassy and crisp. Render the diced guanciale slowly in a pan; strain, keeping both the fat and the crisp cubes.
  2. Toast the peppercorns in a dry pan for 1 minute, until fragrant, then crack them coarsely in a mortar.
  3. Sabayon: in a bowl set over barely simmering water, whisk 4 yolks, the grated Pecorino, 3 tablespoons of warm water and 1 tablespoon of guanciale fat until thick, glossy and 149 °F on a thermometer, about 4 minutes. Take it off the heat and keep it warm, not hot.
  4. Cook the pasta in lightly salted water (fresh pasta needs about 2 minutes). Reserve a ladle of the water.
  5. Off the heat, toss the pasta with 2 tablespoons of guanciale fat, a little pasta water and half of the sabayon until every strand is glossy. Add half of the cracked pepper.
  6. Twirl each portion into a tall, tight nest using a carving fork inside a ladle, and press a small hollow into the top.
Set the nest in a warm shallow bowl and slip a raw yolk into the hollow, brushed with guanciale fat and touched with flaky salt. Spoon the remaining sabayon around, scatter the crisp cubes and cracked pepper, stand a guanciale shard upright, and grate frozen Pecorino over the top like snow at the table.
