Risotto alla Milanese

Creamy Saffron Rice
Rustic version ↓ Michelin version ↓

The story behind the dish

Milan’s golden risotto has a legend: in 1574 a glass-painter’s apprentice working on the windows of the Duomo, who used saffron to color his glass, added it to the rice at his master’s wedding as a joke. The guests loved it.

Whatever its origin, it is the emblem of Milanese cooking: Carnaroli rice toasted in butter, cooked in beef broth, colored with saffron and finished with butter and Grana Padano, often with bone marrow. It is the classic partner of ossobuco.

THE RUSTIC VERSION

The Milanese Kitchen Way

Serves 4 · 35 minutes
Butter, broth, saffron and patience: twenty minutes of stirring for a golden, flowing risotto.

INGREDIENTS

  • 1½ cups Carnaroli or Arborio rice
  • 6 cups hot beef or chicken broth
  • 1 small onion, finely chopped
  • 4 tbsp butter, divided
  • 1 oz beef marrow (optional)
  • ½ cup dry white wine
  • A generous pinch (about ¼ tsp) saffron threads, soaked in ¼ cup hot broth
  • ¾ cup grated Grana Padano or Parmesan
  • Salt

METHOD

  1. Keep the broth at a gentle simmer next to the stove.
  2. Melt 2 tablespoons of butter (and the marrow, if using) in a heavy pan and cook the onion gently for 5 minutes, without browning.
  3. Add the rice and stir for 2 minutes, until the grains are hot and translucent at the edges.
  4. Pour in the wine and stir until it has evaporated.
  5. Add the broth a ladle at a time, stirring often and waiting until each ladle is almost absorbed before adding the next. Cook for 16–18 minutes.
  6. Halfway through, add the saffron and its liquid.
  7. When the rice is tender but still has a slight bite, take the pan off the heat. Add the remaining butter and the cheese, cover for 1 minute, then beat vigorously until creamy and flowing (all’onda). Season and serve at once.
THE MICHELIN 3-STAR VERSION

Saffron, Gold and Marrow

Serves 4 · 1 hour (plus broth)
Aged Carnaroli cooked in a clear veal broth, saffron in two ways, roasted bone marrow and a veal jus, in homage to Gualtiero Marchesi.

INGREDIENTS

  • 1¼ cups aged Carnaroli rice
  • 5 cups clear veal broth, kept hot
  • 4 marrow bones, split lengthwise
  • 1 shallot, very finely chopped
  • ⅓ cup dry white wine
  • Saffron: a generous pinch (about ¼ tsp) of threads infused in 3 tbsp broth, plus a few whole threads
  • 4 tbsp cold butter, cubed, 2 tbsp butter whisked with 2 tsp white wine vinegar (acid butter)
  • ⅔ cup grated 30-month Parmigiano Reggiano
  • ¼ cup reduced veal jus, gold leaf (optional)

TECHNIQUE

  1. Roast the marrow bones at 425 °F for 15 minutes. Scoop out a little marrow and keep it warm.
  2. Toast the rice dry in a heavy pan for 3 minutes until very hot, then add the wine and let it evaporate.
  3. Add a little of the shallot cooked in butter, then the broth a ladle at a time, stirring gently, for 14–15 minutes. Add the saffron infusion halfway.
  4. Off the heat, beat in the cold butter, the acid butter, the cheese and a little marrow. The risotto should spread out like a wave when the plate is tapped.
Spread the risotto in a thin, even disc on a flat white plate by tapping the plate underneath. Place a single square of gold leaf in the center (as Marchesi did) or a few saffron threads, a few drops of veal jus around, and serve the roasted marrow bone on the side with a small spoon.
