Sarmale

Stuffed Cabbage Rolls
Rustic version ↓ Michelin version ↓

The story behind the dish

The wrapped parcel arrived with the Ottomans, whose word sarmak means simply “to wrap”. In the Romanian lands the vine leaf gave way to whole fermented cabbage, lamb gave way to pork, and warm spices softened into dill, thyme and black pepper.

What makes Romanian sarmale unmistakable is the pot itself: small rolls packed tight between layers of smoked pork and sauerkraut, cooked slowly for hours until the sourness of the cabbage and the smoke of the meat become one flavor. They are the dish of Christmas, of weddings and of every family table that matters.

THE RUSTIC VERSION

Grandmother’s Pot

Serves 8 (about 40 rolls) · 4 hours
The way it is still made in Romanian villages: a big pot, sour cabbage, smoked pork and patience.

INGREDIENTS

  • 1 large head of whole fermented (sour) cabbage, about 4½ lb
  • 2¼ lb ground pork shoulder (or half pork, half beef)
  • 2 medium onions, finely chopped
  • 3 tbsp sunflower oil or lard
  • ½ cup short-grain rice, rinsed
  • 2 tbsp tomato paste
  • 2 tsp salt, 1 tsp black pepper, 1 tsp sweet paprika
  • 1 tsp dried thyme and 2 tbsp chopped fresh dill
  • 10 oz smoked bacon or smoked pork ribs, in pieces
  • 2 bay leaves and 1 tsp black peppercorns
  • 1⅔ cups tomato juice or tomato purée
  • To serve: sour cream, mămăligă (polenta) and pickled hot peppers

METHOD

  1. Separate the cabbage leaves and taste one. If it is very salty, soak the leaves in cold water for 30 minutes to 2 hours. Shave the thick central rib flat with a small knife and cut very large leaves in half. Finely shred the small inner leaves and any torn pieces.
  2. Cook the onions in the oil over medium heat for about 8 minutes, until soft and golden. Add the rice and stir for 2 minutes, then stir in the tomato paste for 1 minute. Let it cool.
  3. In a large bowl, mix the meat with the onion and rice, salt, pepper, paprika, thyme, dill and 3–4 tablespoons of water. Knead with your hand for 2 minutes, until sticky.
  4. Place a heaped tablespoon of filling at the base of a leaf. Fold the base over it, roll halfway, tuck in both sides and finish rolling, pushing the end inside. Keep the rolls small, about the size of two fingers.
  5. Cover the bottom of a large heavy pot with half of the shredded cabbage and a few pieces of smoked pork. Arrange the rolls snugly in circles, layering them with the rest of the smoked pork, bay leaves and peppercorns. Finish with the remaining shredded cabbage.
  6. Pour in the tomato juice, then enough water (or water mixed with a little cabbage brine) to just cover. Set a heatproof plate on top so the rolls stay submerged.
  7. Bring slowly to a gentle simmer, cover, and bake at 325 °F for about 3 hours (or simmer on the lowest heat on the stove). Add a little water if the level drops. They are ready when the rice is completely tender.
  8. Rest for 15 minutes before serving with sour cream, hot polenta and a pickled pepper.
Tip. Sarmale are even better the next day, reheated slowly in their own juice. They also freeze well.
THE MICHELIN 3-STAR VERSION

Sarmale, Smoke and Ferment

Serves 4 (12 rolls) · 6 hours, best started a day ahead
Three small lacquered rolls of heritage pork, with smoked sour cream, crisp polenta and a jus built from the smoked ribs.

INGREDIENTS

  • 12 tender inner leaves of fermented cabbage, plus ⅓ cup cabbage brine
  • Filling: 14 oz Mangalica or other heritage pork shoulder, hand-chopped
  • 3 oz smoked pork belly, in very small dice
  • 3 tbsp short-grain rice, boiled for 5 minutes and drained
  • 1 shallot, finely diced and softened in butter
  • 1 tsp chopped dill, ½ tsp chopped thyme, 1 tsp salt, white pepper
  • Jus: 1 lb smoked pork ribs, 1 onion, 1 tbsp tomato paste, 1 quart chicken stock, 2 tbsp cold butter
  • Polenta: 1¼ cups milk, ⅓ cup fine cornmeal, 1½ tbsp butter, ¼ cup grated aged sheep’s cheese or Parmesan
  • Dill oil: 1 oz dill, ⅓ cup grapeseed oil
  • ⅔ cup thick sour cream and a pinch of smoked salt
  • To finish: dill tips and crisp crumbs of smoked bacon

TECHNIQUE

  1. Jus: roast the ribs and halved onion at 425 °F for 30 minutes. Transfer to a pot, stir in the tomato paste, add the stock and simmer gently for 3 hours. Strain, then reduce to about ¾ cup of glossy jus. Season it with a spoonful of cabbage brine instead of salt.
  2. Filling: mix the chopped pork, smoked belly, rice, shallot, herbs, salt and white pepper. Poach a small ball in simmering water and taste; adjust the seasoning. Chill for 30 minutes.
  3. Shave the leaf ribs completely flat and trim the leaves into even rectangles. Roll about 1 oz of filling into each, forming 12 tight, identical cylinders.
  4. Pack the rolls in a single layer in a small ovenproof dish. Pour over half the jus and the remaining brine, cover tightly and cook at 285 °F for 3 hours.
  5. Polenta: whisk the cornmeal into simmering milk and cook for 20 minutes, stirring often. Beat in the butter and cheese, spread ⅝ inch thick on a lined tray and chill. Cut into small cubes and fry in butter until crisp on all sides.
  6. Dill oil: blend the dill with the oil for 2 minutes, then strain through a coffee filter. Season the sour cream with a pinch of smoked salt and beat until just thick enough to hold a shape.
  7. Lift the rolls into a wide pan with the remaining jus and spoon it over them repeatedly over low heat until they are lacquered. Whisk the cold butter into the jus.
Three lacquered rolls in a line on a warm plate, a quenelle of smoked sour cream beside them, three crisp polenta cubes, the buttered jus dotted with dill oil, and a scattering of bacon crumbs and dill tips. Pour a Fetească Neagră.
