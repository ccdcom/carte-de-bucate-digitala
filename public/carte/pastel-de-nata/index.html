Pastel de Nata

Custard Tart
Rustic version ↓ Michelin version ↓

The story behind the dish

Lisbon’s custard tart was born behind the walls of the Jerónimos Monastery in Belém, where egg whites starched the monks’ robes and the leftover yolks became sweets. When the monastery closed in 1834, the recipe passed to a sugar refinery next door, and in 1837 the Fábrica de Pastéis de Belém opened its doors.

The original formula is still kept in a locked room. Everywhere else, the ideal is the same: shatteringly crisp spiral pastry, a just-set custard, and the black, blistered spots that only a fiercely hot oven can paint.

THE RUSTIC VERSION

The Lisbon Café Tart

Makes 12 tarts · 1½ hours
With good all-butter puff pastry and a very hot oven, these come remarkably close to the ones in Belém.

INGREDIENTS

  • 1 sheet (about 10 oz) all-butter puff pastry
  • Syrup: 1 cup sugar, ½ cup water, 1 cinnamon stick, 1 wide strip of lemon peel
  • Custard: ¼ cup all-purpose flour, 1¼ cups whole milk, 6 egg yolks
  • A little soft butter for the pan
  • Ground cinnamon and powdered sugar, to serve

METHOD

  1. Heat the oven as high as it goes, ideally 500–550 °F, with a rack near the top. Lightly butter a 12-cup muffin pan.
  2. Roll the pastry tightly into a log from the short side and cut it into 12 equal discs. Set each disc cut side up in a cup of the pan. With a wet thumb, press from the center outwards and up the sides until the pastry rises just above the rim and is thinnest at the base. Chill for 15 minutes.
  3. Boil the sugar, water, cinnamon and lemon peel for 3 minutes, until syrupy (220 °F).
  4. Whisk the flour with ¼ cup of the milk until smooth. Bring the rest of the milk to a simmer, whisk in the flour paste and cook for 1–2 minutes until thickened.
  5. Remove the cinnamon and lemon from the syrup and pour the hot syrup into the milk in a thin stream, whisking all the time. Cool for 5 minutes, whisk in the yolks and strain into a jug.
  6. Fill the pastry cases three-quarters full. Bake for 12–15 minutes, until the pastry is deep golden and the custard has dark blistered spots.
  7. Cool for 10 minutes, then dust with cinnamon and powdered sugar. Eat them the same day, ideally still warm.
Tip. If your oven will not blister the tops, finish them for a few seconds with a kitchen blowtorch.
THE MICHELIN 3-STAR VERSION

Belém After Dark

Makes 12 (serves 6) · 1 day (mostly resting)
Hand-laminated spiral pastry, a vanilla and Ceylon cinnamon custard, tawny Port and cultured cream: one perfect tart, served minutes from the oven.

INGREDIENTS

  • Pastry: 2 cups bread flour, ½ tsp salt, ⅔ cup cold water
  • 7 oz (1¾ sticks) high-fat unsalted butter, softened to a spreadable paste
  • Custard: 1¼ cups whole milk, ⅓ cup heavy cream, 3 tbsp all-purpose flour
  • 1 vanilla bean, split; 1 Ceylon cinnamon stick; peel of 1 lemon
  • 7 egg yolks
  • Syrup: 1 cup + 2 tbsp sugar, ½ cup water
  • To finish: ¾ cup tawny Port, 1 tbsp sugar, ½ cup crème fraîche, 1 tsp powdered sugar, ground Ceylon cinnamon, flaky sea salt

TECHNIQUE

  1. Pastry: mix the flour, salt and water into a soft dough, knead for 3 minutes and rest for 15 minutes. On a floured surface, roll it out paper-thin into a rectangle about 20 × 16 inches. Spread the soft butter over it in an even, thin layer and roll it up from the short side into a tight log. Wrap and chill for at least 2 hours, ideally overnight.
  2. Warm the milk and cream with the vanilla, cinnamon and lemon peel, then leave to infuse for 30 minutes. Boil the sugar and water to 223 °F.
  3. Whisk the flour with a little of the infused milk, then whisk it into the rest and cook until thickened. Strain out the aromatics, pour in the hot syrup in a thin stream, cool to lukewarm and whisk in the yolks. Pass through a fine sieve and rest for 1 hour so the bubbles rise.
  4. Cut the log into ¾-inch discs and press them into chilled nata tins (or a muffin pan), spiraling the pastry thinly up to just above the rim. Chill for 30 minutes.
  5. Reduce the Port with the sugar to a syrup that coats a spoon. Whip the crème fraîche with the powdered sugar until it just holds its shape.
  6. Fill the cases three-quarters full and bake on the top rack at the oven’s highest setting (about 550 °F, convection if possible) for 10–12 minutes, until the edges shatter and the surface is blistered black in places.
One warm tart on a matte stone plate, dusted with cinnamon through a half-moon stencil and touched with three flakes of sea salt; a quenelle of crème fraîche to the side and three drops of Port reduction. Serve within fifteen minutes of the oven.
