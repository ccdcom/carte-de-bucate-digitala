Papanași

Cheese Doughnuts
Rustic version ↓ Michelin version ↓

The story behind the dish

Papanași were born in pastoral kitchens, where fresh cow’s cheese was always at hand. The oldest form is boiled: soft cheese dumplings served with sour cream and sugar. The modern version is fried: a golden ring crowned with a small ball, like a hat.

Heaped with thick sour cream and sour cherry jam, papanași are the dessert every Romanian orders after a long meal, with the excuse that “it is mostly cheese.”

THE RUSTIC VERSION

Mountain Cabin Papanași

Makes 6 · 45 minutes
Fried in hot oil until golden, eaten warm with sour cream and cherry jam.

INGREDIENTS

  • 1 lb fresh farmer’s cheese (dry cottage or quark), well drained
  • 2 eggs
  • 3 tbsp sugar and a pinch of salt
  • Zest of 1 lemon and 1 tsp vanilla extract
  • ½ tsp baking soda
  • About 1⅓ cups all-purpose flour
  • Sunflower oil for deep frying
  • To serve: 1 cup thick sour cream and ¾ cup sour cherry or blueberry jam, powdered sugar

METHOD

  1. Mash the cheese with a fork until smooth. Mix in the eggs, sugar, salt, lemon zest, vanilla and baking soda.
  2. Add the flour gradually and knead lightly, just until you have a soft dough that barely sticks to your hands.
  3. Divide into 6 large and 6 small pieces. Shape the large ones into thick discs and make a hole in the center with a floured finger, like a doughnut. Roll the small pieces into balls.
  4. Heat the oil to 340 °F. Fry the rings and balls a few at a time for 3–4 minutes per side, until deep golden and cooked through. Drain on paper towels.
  5. Place a ring on each plate, spoon over sour cream and jam, set a ball on top and add more jam. Dust with powdered sugar and serve warm.
THE MICHELIN 3-STAR VERSION

Papanași, Cherry and Cultured Cream

Serves 4 · 4 hours
A delicate fried cheese ring, sour cherries cooked in their own juice, smântână ice cream and a burnt-honey tuile.

INGREDIENTS

  • 9 oz fresh sheep’s or cow’s ricotta, hung overnight in cloth
  • 1 egg yolk, 1 tbsp sugar, zest of ½ orange, pinch of salt
  • ¾ cup all-purpose flour and ½ tsp baking powder
  • Grapeseed oil for frying
  • Cherries: 1½ cups pitted sour cherries, 3 tbsp sugar, 1 tbsp kirsch, 1 tsp cornstarch
  • Ice cream: 1 cup sour cream, ½ cup whole milk, ⅓ cup sugar, 1 tbsp glucose or honey, juice of ½ lemon
  • Tuile: 2 tbsp honey, 2 tbsp butter, 2 tbsp sugar, 2 tbsp flour
  • To finish: crème fraîche, lemon verbena or mint

TECHNIQUE

  1. Ice cream: warm the milk, sugar and glucose until dissolved, cool, whisk into the sour cream with the lemon juice and churn. Freeze.
  2. Cherries: macerate with the sugar for 1 hour, then simmer for 3 minutes. Thicken the juice with the cornstarch and finish with kirsch.
  3. Tuile: melt the honey, butter and sugar, cook until dark amber, stir in the flour, cool. Spread thinly on a silicone mat and bake at 340 °F for 6–7 minutes. Shape while warm.
  4. Dough: mix the cheese, yolk, sugar, zest and salt, then fold in the flour and baking powder. Rest 20 minutes.
  5. Shape 4 small rings and 4 small spheres. Fry at 340 °F for about 3 minutes, until evenly golden. Drain and roll the spheres in sugar.
A warm ring in the center of the plate filled with glossy cherries, a quenelle of sour-cream ice cream on top, the sugared sphere beside it, a dot of crème fraîche, a shard of honey tuile and a few verbena leaves.
