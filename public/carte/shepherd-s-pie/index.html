Shepherd’s Pie

Rustic version ↓ Michelin version ↓

The story behind the dish

When potatoes became the food of the poor in 18th-century Britain, thrifty cooks covered leftover roast meat with mashed potato and baked it. Made with lamb or mutton, the food of shepherds, it became shepherd’s pie; made with beef, cottage pie.

Today it is made with fresh ground lamb in a rich gravy with vegetables, under a golden, ridged mash. It is the comfort food of every British home and pub in winter.

THE RUSTIC VERSION

The Village Pub Pie

Serves 6 · 1½ hours
Minced lamb in rich gravy under a fluffy, golden potato crust.

INGREDIENTS

  • 2 lb ground lamb
  • 1 tbsp oil
  • 1 onion, 2 carrots and 1 celery stalk, finely diced
  • 2 garlic cloves, crushed
  • 2 tbsp tomato paste and 1 tbsp flour
  • 1 tbsp Worcestershire sauce
  • 1½ cups lamb or beef stock
  • 2 sprigs rosemary and thyme
  • 1 cup frozen peas
  • Mash: 3 lb floury potatoes, ½ cup butter, ½ cup warm milk, 1 egg yolk, salt and pepper

METHOD

  1. Brown the lamb in the oil in a large pan, breaking it up. Spoon off excess fat.
  2. Add the onion, carrots, celery and garlic and cook for 8 minutes.
  3. Stir in the tomato paste and flour, then the Worcestershire sauce, stock and herbs. Simmer for 30 minutes until thick and rich. Add the peas and season.
  4. Boil the potatoes until tender, drain and mash with the butter, milk and yolk. Season.
  5. Spoon the lamb into a baking dish, cover with the mash and rough the top with a fork.
  6. Bake at 400 °F for 25–30 minutes, until golden and bubbling at the edges.
THE MICHELIN 3-STAR VERSION

Shepherd’s Pie, Slow Lamb

Serves 6 · 6 hours
Braised lamb shoulder in a deep lamb sauce, glazed root vegetables, a pommes purée crown and crisp lamb belly.

INGREDIENTS

  • 2½ lb lamb shoulder on the bone
  • 1 onion, 1 carrot, 1 leek, garlic head, rosemary, 1 cup red wine, 3 cups lamb stock
  • Glazed vegetables: baby carrots, small turnips and pearl onions, cooked in butter and a little stock
  • Pommes purée: 2 lb Yukon Gold potatoes, ¾ cup butter, warm milk, nutmeg
  • 8 oz lamb belly, slow-roasted and crisped
  • Mint gremolata: mint, parsley, lemon zest, garlic
  • Peas and pea shoots

TECHNIQUE

  1. Brown the shoulder, add the vegetables, wine and stock, cover and braise at 300 °F for 4 hours. Pull the meat into large strands. Strain and reduce the liquid to a rich sauce; fold half into the meat.
  2. Make a very smooth potato purée and fill a piping bag.
  3. Glaze the vegetables until tender and shiny.
  4. Fill small cast-iron dishes or rings with lamb, pipe the purée on top in neat peaks and color under a hot broiler or with a blowtorch.
  5. Cut the crisp lamb belly into small pieces.
The little pie served in its dish on a board, with a side plate of glazed vegetables, peas and crisp belly, and the remaining sauce poured over the vegetables. A sprinkle of mint gremolata on the pie.
