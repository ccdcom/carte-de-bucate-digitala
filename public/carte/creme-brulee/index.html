🇫🇷 France

Crème Brûlée

Custard Dessert
Rustic version ↓ Michelin version ↓

The story behind the dish

France, England and Spain all claim this dessert. A French recipe for “crème brûlée” appears in 1691 in the cookbook of François Massialot, cook to the Duke of Orléans. Cambridge has its “burnt cream,” and Catalonia its crema catalana.

The pleasure is in the contrast: a cold, silky vanilla custard under a thin glass of caramelized sugar that cracks under the spoon. It became the star of French restaurants in the 1980s and has never left.

THE RUSTIC VERSION

The Bistro Classic

Serves 6 · 1 hour, plus 4 hours chilling
Cream, vanilla, yolks and sugar, baked slowly and burnt at the last minute.

INGREDIENTS

  • 2 cups heavy cream
  • ½ cup whole milk
  • 1 vanilla bean, split (or 2 tsp vanilla extract)
  • 6 egg yolks
  • ½ cup sugar, plus about 6 tbsp for the tops

METHOD

  1. Heat the oven to 300 °F. Set six 5-oz ramekins in a deep baking pan.
  2. Heat the cream and milk with the vanilla bean (seeds scraped in) until steaming. Leave to infuse for 15 minutes.
  3. Whisk the yolks with the sugar until just combined, without making foam. Slowly whisk in the warm cream. Strain.
  4. Divide among the ramekins. Pour hot water into the pan to come halfway up the sides.
  5. Bake for 30–40 minutes, until set at the edges but still trembling in the center.
  6. Cool, then refrigerate for at least 4 hours.
  7. Just before serving, sprinkle each with an even layer of sugar and caramelize with a blowtorch until deep amber. Wait 1 minute for the caramel to harden.
THE MICHELIN 3-STAR VERSION

Crème Brûlée, Tahitian Vanilla and Raspberry

Serves 6 · 1 day
A shallow, very silky Tahitian vanilla custard, a perfectly even glass of caramel, raspberries and a raspberry sorbet.

INGREDIENTS

  • Custard: 2 cups heavy cream, ½ cup whole milk, 2 Tahitian vanilla beans, 7 egg yolks, ⅓ cup sugar
  • Raw cane sugar or a mix of white and light brown sugar for the tops
  • Sorbet: 2 cups raspberries, ½ cup sugar, ⅓ cup water, juice of ½ lemon
  • Fresh raspberries, lemon verbena, a madeleine or almond tuile on the side

TECHNIQUE

  1. Infuse the cream and milk with the vanilla overnight in the fridge for maximum flavor.
  2. Warm the infused cream to 140 °F, whisk into the yolks and sugar, strain, and let rest 1 hour so the bubbles disappear.
  3. Pour into wide, shallow gratin dishes (about ¾ inch deep). Bake at 210 °F (no water bath needed at this low temperature) for 40–50 minutes, until just set.
  4. Chill for at least 4 hours.
  5. Sorbet: dissolve the sugar in the water, blend with the raspberries and lemon, sieve and churn.
  6. Caramelize in two thin layers of sugar with a blowtorch for an even, glassy crust.
