Fish and Chips

Rustic version ↓ Michelin version ↓

The story behind the dish

Fried fish came to London with Sephardic Jewish refugees from Portugal and Spain, and chips came from the north of England and Belgium. Sometime in the 1860s the two met: Joseph Malin in London and John Lees in Lancashire both claim the first chip shop.

By the 20th century, fish and chips fed the working class through two world wars; it was one of the few foods never rationed. Cod or haddock in a crisp batter, thick chips, salt, malt vinegar and mushy peas, eaten from paper by the sea.

THE RUSTIC VERSION

The Seaside Chippy Supper

Serves 4 · 1½ hours
Beer-battered haddock, thick chips cooked twice, and mushy peas.

INGREDIENTS

  • 4 thick skinless haddock or cod fillets (6 oz each)
  • Batter: 1½ cups all-purpose flour, 1 tsp baking powder, 1 tsp salt, 1¼ cups very cold beer or sparkling water
  • Extra flour for dusting
  • Chips: 2½ lb floury potatoes (Maris Piper or Russet), cut into ½-inch batons
  • Beef dripping or vegetable oil for deep-frying
  • Mushy peas: 1 lb frozen peas, 2 tbsp butter, mint, salt
  • Malt vinegar, salt, lemon wedges, tartar sauce

METHOD

  1. Rinse and dry the potato batons. Fry at 285 °F for 8–10 minutes, until soft but not colored. Drain and let cool.
  2. Make the mushy peas: cook the peas for 4 minutes, drain and crush roughly with the butter, a little mint and salt. Keep warm.
  3. Whisk the flour, baking powder and salt with the cold beer into a smooth batter about as thick as heavy cream. Use straight away.
  4. Heat the oil to 350 °F. Pat the fish dry, season, dust in flour, dip in batter and lower gently into the oil, away from you. Fry for 5–7 minutes, until golden and crisp. Drain on a rack.
  5. Raise the oil to 375 °F and fry the chips again for 3–4 minutes until golden and crisp. Salt immediately.
  6. Serve with salt, malt vinegar, lemon, mushy peas and tartar sauce.
THE MICHELIN 3-STAR VERSION

Fish, Chips and Vinegar

Serves 4 · 4 hours
Turbot in a glass-crisp batter, triple-cooked chips, pea purée with mint, and a malt vinegar gel and powder.

INGREDIENTS

  • 4 turbot or halibut portions (5 oz each), skinless
  • Batter: 1 cup rice flour, ½ cup all-purpose flour, 1 tsp baking powder, 1 cup ice-cold lager, 2 tbsp vodka (for crispness)
  • Triple-cooked chips: 4 large potatoes, cut into neat blocks
  • Pea purée: 2 cups peas, ½ cup stock, 2 tbsp butter, mint
  • Malt vinegar gel: ½ cup malt vinegar, 2 tbsp sugar, ½ tsp agar-agar
  • Tartare: 2 tbsp each capers, cornichons and shallot, chopped, ⅓ cup mayonnaise, dill
  • Pea shoots, lemon, sea salt flakes

TECHNIQUE

  1. Chips: simmer the potato blocks until almost falling apart, chill on a rack until dry, fry at 265 °F for 5 minutes, chill again, then fry at 375 °F to order until deeply golden.
  2. Purée: cook the peas in the stock, blend with the butter and mint until silky; pass through a sieve.
  3. Gel: boil the vinegar with sugar and agar, set, then blend smooth.
  4. Mix the tartare ingredients.
  5. Whisk the batter ingredients at the last moment, dip the dried, seasoned fish and fry at 350 °F for 4–5 minutes until crisp and pale gold.
A swoosh of pea purée, the battered turbot cut in two to show the pearly flesh, three neat triple-cooked chips stacked like logs, dots of vinegar gel, a quenelle of tartare and pea shoots, with sea salt and a lemon cheek.
