🇵🇱 Poland

Baltic Herring Trilogy

Rustic version ↓ Michelin version ↓

The story behind the dish

Herring has been Poland’s most faithful fish for centuries, carried inland in salted barrels along medieval trade routes and eaten by monks on fast days. It appears at Wigilia, the Christmas Eve supper, as a symbol of prosperity.

Three classic preparations show its range: in oil with onions and allspice, in sour cream with apple and dill, and in a deeper, sweeter marinade. Served with dark rye and a small glass of cold vodka.

THE RUSTIC VERSION

Three Herrings for Wigilia

Serves 8 as a starter · 45 minutes, plus 1 day marinating
Herring in oil, herring in sour cream with apple, and herring in a sweet-and-sour tomato marinade.

INGREDIENTS

  • 1½ lb salted herring fillets (matjas), soaked in milk or water for 2 hours if very salty, cut into 1½-inch pieces
  • In oil: 1 onion, sliced and briefly blanched in vinegar water, 1 cup cold-pressed rapeseed or sunflower oil, 2 bay leaves, 5 allspice berries, 10 peppercorns
  • In cream: 1 cup sour cream, 1 tart apple, diced, ½ red onion, finely chopped, 1 tbsp chopped dill, 1 tsp lemon juice
  • In tomato: ½ cup tomato paste thinned with ¼ cup water, 2 tbsp oil, 1 tbsp sugar, 1 tbsp vinegar, 1 onion, sliced, ½ tsp sweet paprika
  • Dark rye bread and boiled potatoes, to serve

METHOD

  1. Divide the herring pieces into three portions.
  2. In oil: layer the herring in a jar with the blanched onion, bay leaves and spices, cover with oil and refrigerate for at least 24 hours.
  3. In cream: mix the sour cream with the apple, red onion, dill and lemon juice, fold in the herring and chill for a few hours.
  4. In tomato: soften the onion in oil, add the tomato paste, water, sugar, vinegar and paprika and simmer 5 minutes. Cool, fold in the herring and chill overnight.
  5. Serve the three side by side with dark rye bread, boiled potatoes and a glass of cold vodka.
THE MICHELIN 3-STAR VERSION

Herring, Three Ways

Serves 4 as a starter · 1 day
Cured herring with oil and onion ash, herring tartare with apple and sour cream, and torched herring with beet and horseradish.

INGREDIENTS

  • 6 fresh herring fillets, cured in 2 tbsp salt and 1 tbsp sugar for 3 hours, then marinated in light vinegar for 12 hours (or high-quality matjas fillets)
  • Onion ash: 1 onion, burnt black in the oven and ground; cold-pressed rapeseed oil
  • Tartare: ½ green apple, 1 tbsp shallot, chives, ¼ cup thick sour cream, dill oil
  • Beet: 1 roasted beet, puréed and a few cubes pickled; fresh horseradish
  • Rye crumble: dark rye bread toasted with butter and caraway
  • Allspice, borage flowers

TECHNIQUE

  1. First way: cut neat pieces of herring, brush with rapeseed oil, dust with onion ash and a crush of allspice.
  2. Second way: dice a fillet finely, mix with apple, shallot and chives; serve on sour cream with dill oil.
  3. Third way: brush a fillet with a little honey, torch the skin until blistered, and serve on beet purée with grated horseradish.
  4. Make the rye crumble.
On a long plate, from left to right: the ash-dusted herring with oil, the tartare on sour cream, the torched herring on beet purée with horseradish snow, linked by a line of rye crumble and borage flowers. A frozen shot of vodka alongside.
