🇧🇪 Belgium

Carbonnade Flamande

Beef and Beer Stew
Rustic version ↓ Michelin version ↓

The story behind the dish

Flanders’ beef and beer stew is the Belgian answer to beef bourguignon: where France uses wine, Belgium uses its brown and sour ales. Beef is browned, then slowly braised with onions, dark beer, a little vinegar and brown sugar, and a slice of bread spread with mustard that melts into the sauce.

The result is deep, sweet-sour and dark, served with fries or potatoes and, of course, the same beer as the one in the pot. It is the dish of Flemish homes and of every estaminet, the traditional café.

THE RUSTIC VERSION

The Flemish Café Stew

Serves 6 · 3½ hours
Beef, onions and dark ale, slowly simmered under a slice of mustard bread.

INGREDIENTS

  • 3 lb beef chuck, in 2-inch pieces
  • 3 tbsp butter or lard
  • 3 large onions, sliced
  • 2 tbsp flour
  • 2 bottles (about 3 cups) Belgian brown ale (Flemish red-brown or Leffe Brune)
  • 1 tbsp brown sugar and 1 tbsp cider vinegar
  • 2 bay leaves and 3 sprigs thyme
  • 1 thick slice of bread, spread with 2 tbsp mustard
  • Salt, pepper; fries or boiled potatoes to serve

METHOD

  1. Season the beef and brown it well in batches in the butter. Set aside.
  2. Cook the onions in the same pot for 15 minutes, until golden.
  3. Stir in the flour, then add the beer, scraping the bottom. Return the beef with the sugar, vinegar, bay leaves and thyme.
  4. Lay the mustard bread on top, mustard side down. Cover and simmer gently for 2½–3 hours, stirring once or twice, until the meat is very tender and the bread has dissolved into the sauce.
  5. Season to taste and serve with fries and a glass of the same beer.
THE MICHELIN 3-STAR VERSION

Carbonnade, Cheek and Beer

Serves 4 · 1 day
Beef cheek braised in Flemish red ale, lacquered with its sauce, with onion three ways, gingerbread crumb and smoked potato purée.

INGREDIENTS

  • 4 beef cheeks, trimmed
  • 3 cups Flemish red ale (Rodenbach or similar), 2 cups veal stock
  • 1 onion, 1 carrot, thyme, bay leaf, 1 tbsp brown sugar
  • Onions: 4 pearl onions, glazed; 1 onion cooked into a black purée; crisp onion rings
  • Gingerbread crumb: 2 slices Belgian peperkoek or gingerbread, dried and crumbled with butter
  • Smoked potato purée: 1 lb potatoes, ½ cup butter, milk, smoked salt
  • Mustard seeds, pickled; chervil

TECHNIQUE

  1. Sear the cheeks, add the vegetables, sugar, beer and stock, cover and braise at 285 °F for 4 hours. Cool in the liquid overnight.
  2. Next day lift out the cheeks, strain and reduce the liquid to a dark, glossy glaze.
  3. Make the three onion garnishes and the gingerbread crumb.
  4. Make the potato purée and season with smoked salt.
  5. Warm the cheeks in the glaze, basting until lacquered.
A swoosh of smoked potato purée, a lacquered beef cheek on it, a line of gingerbread crumb, glazed pearl onions, a dot of black onion purée, crisp onion rings, pickled mustard seeds and chervil, with extra glaze at the table.
