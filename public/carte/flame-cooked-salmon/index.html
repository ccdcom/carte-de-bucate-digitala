🇫🇮 Finland

Flame-Cooked Salmon

Loimulohi
Rustic version ↓ Michelin version ↓

The story behind the dish

Loimulohi is Finland’s great outdoor dish: a side of salmon nailed to a wooden board and set upright beside an open fire, cooking slowly in the radiant heat and wood smoke. It is a familiar sight at summer markets, festivals and lakeside cabins.

The fish turns silky and lightly smoky, with a golden glaze from the salt and sugar rubbed on beforehand. In Helsinki, chefs serve it with spruce-tip oil, pickled fennel and a toasted rye crumble.

THE RUSTIC VERSION

Lakeside Loimulohi

Serves 6–8 · 1½ hours, plus building the fire
A side of salmon cooked on a plank beside the campfire, or on a cedar plank on the grill at home.

INGREDIENTS

  • 1 side of salmon (about 3 lb), skin on, pin bones removed
  • 2 tbsp coarse salt and 1 tbsp sugar
  • 1 untreated hardwood plank (alder or birch) about 6 inches longer than the fish, soaked in water for 1 hour, and stainless steel nails or wooden pegs
  • Lemon and dill
  • New potatoes with butter and dill, cucumber salad, to serve

METHOD

  1. Rub the flesh with salt and sugar and leave for 30 minutes.
  2. Nail or peg the salmon, skin side against the plank, at the top, middle and bottom.
  3. Build a good wood fire and let it burn down to strong, steady flames.
  4. Stand the plank at an angle about 12–20 inches from the flames, head end down, flesh facing the fire.
  5. Cook for 30–50 minutes, depending on the heat, turning the plank upside down halfway and moving it closer or farther so the fish cooks evenly without burning. It is done when the flesh flakes and is opaque (about 130 °F in the thickest part).
  6. Serve on the plank with lemon, dill, buttered potatoes and cucumber salad.
Tip. At home, cook it on a soaked cedar or alder plank on a covered grill at 350–375 °F for 20–25 minutes.
THE MICHELIN 3-STAR VERSION

Fire, Spruce and Rye

Serves 4 · 2 hours
Flame-kissed salmon belly, pickled fennel, spruce-tip oil, buttermilk sauce and a toasted rye crumble, as served in Helsinki.

INGREDIENTS

  • 4 portions salmon (5 oz each), lightly cured with salt and sugar for 20 minutes
  • Pickled fennel: 1 fennel bulb, shaved thin, in ½ cup white wine vinegar, ¼ cup sugar, ½ cup water
  • Spruce-tip oil: a handful of young spruce tips (or fresh dill and parsley), ½ cup oil, blended and strained
  • Buttermilk sauce: ½ cup buttermilk, 2 tbsp brown butter, salt, lemon
  • Rye crumble: 2 slices dark rye, toasted with butter and a little brown sugar
  • Smoked salt, dill, pickled mustard seeds

TECHNIQUE

  1. Pickle the fennel for at least 1 hour.
  2. Make the spruce oil and the rye crumble.
  3. Warm the buttermilk gently with the brown butter and season; it should look slightly broken.
  4. Cook the salmon close to wood embers or under a very hot broiler, flesh facing the heat, just until the surface is golden and the center is still translucent (120 °F). Or torch the surface of slow-baked salmon (250 °F for 15 minutes).
