🇮🇹 Italy

Panettone

Italian Christmas Cake
Rustic version ↓ Michelin version ↓

The story behind the dish

Milan’s Christmas bread has many legends, the most famous about a kitchen boy named Toni who saved a ruined dessert at the Sforza court: “pan de Toni.” What is certain is that rich sweet breads were baked in Milan for Christmas since the Middle Ages, and that in the 20th century bakers like Motta and Alemagna made the tall, domed panettone a national icon.

A real panettone takes days: a natural sourdough refreshed again and again, butter, egg yolks, candied orange and raisins, a slow rise and a final night hanging upside down so the airy crumb does not collapse. It is the cousin of the Romanian cozonac and the German stollen.

THE RUSTIC VERSION

A Home Baker’s Panettone

Makes one 2-lb panettone · 2 days (mostly rising)
A simplified version with commercial yeast and an overnight rise, tall and fragrant.

INGREDIENTS

  • Starter: ½ cup bread flour, ¼ cup warm milk, 1 tsp instant yeast
  • Dough: 3 cups bread flour, ⅓ cup sugar, 4 egg yolks + 1 whole egg, ⅓ cup warm milk, ½ cup soft butter, 1 tsp salt
  • Zest of 1 orange and 1 lemon, 1 tbsp honey, 2 tsp vanilla extract
  • ¾ cup raisins, soaked in rum or warm water, drained
  • ½ cup candied orange peel, diced
  • A 6-inch paper panettone mold

METHOD

  1. Mix the starter, cover and leave for 1 hour until bubbly.
  2. Add the flour, sugar, eggs, milk, honey and vanilla to the starter and knead (ideally in a mixer) for 10 minutes until smooth and elastic.
  3. Add the butter a little at a time, then the salt and zests, and knead until the dough is glossy and stretches into a thin window.
  4. Mix in the raisins and candied peel. Cover and refrigerate overnight.
  5. Shape into a tight ball, place in the paper mold and leave somewhere warm for 3–5 hours, until the dough rises just above the rim.
  6. Cut a cross on top and put a knob of butter in the center. Bake at 350 °F for 45–55 minutes (internal temperature 195 °F). Cover with foil if it browns too fast.
  7. Push two skewers through the base, then hang it upside down between two pots until completely cool.
THE MICHELIN 3-STAR VERSION

Panettone, Warm and Torn

Serves 6 · 45 minutes (plus a 3-day panettone)
A slice of naturally leavened panettone toasted in brown butter, with candied citrus, mascarpone and Vin Santo zabaglione.

INGREDIENTS

  • 1 artisan sourdough panettone (homemade over 3 days, or from a great pastry shop)
  • 4 tbsp brown butter
  • Zabaglione: 4 egg yolks, ¼ cup sugar, ½ cup Vin Santo
  • Mascarpone cream: 1 cup mascarpone, ¼ cup heavy cream, 1 tbsp honey, vanilla bean seeds
  • Candied citrus: peel of 1 orange and 1 lemon in fine julienne, ½ cup sugar, ½ cup water
  • Candied chestnut pieces or toasted almonds, orange zest

TECHNIQUE

  1. Candy the citrus peel: blanch twice, then simmer in the sugar and water for 20 minutes until translucent.
  2. Whip the mascarpone with the cream, honey and vanilla until just firm.
  3. Cut the panettone into thick wedges and toast in the brown butter over medium heat until golden and crisp on the cut sides.
  4. Zabaglione: just before serving, whisk the yolks, sugar and Vin Santo over simmering water for 4–5 minutes, until tripled and warm.
