Pizza

Margherita, the Neapolitan Classic
Rustic version ↓ Michelin version ↓

The story behind the dish

Pizza was born in the crowded streets of 18th-century Naples, where the lazzaroni, the city’s working poor, ate flatbreads topped with tomatoes, a fruit the European elites still distrusted. In 1889, the story goes, pizzaiolo Raffaele Esposito made one in the colors of the flag for Queen Margherita: red tomato, white mozzarella, green basil.

The real thing is simple and demanding: a soft, airy dough, a blistered rim, and a few minutes in a very hot oven. In 2017 UNESCO added the art of the Neapolitan pizzaiolo to its list of intangible heritage.

THE RUSTIC VERSION

Margherita at Home

Makes 4 pizzas · 30 minutes, plus 24 hours rising
A slow-risen dough and the hottest oven you have: the best Neapolitan-style pizza a home kitchen can make.

INGREDIENTS

  • Dough: 4 cups “00” flour (or bread flour), 1⅔ cups cool water, 2 tsp salt, ¼ tsp instant yeast
  • 1 can (14 oz) San Marzano tomatoes, crushed by hand, with a pinch of salt
  • 9 oz fresh mozzarella (fior di latte), torn and drained
  • Fresh basil leaves
  • Extra-virgin olive oil

METHOD

  1. Mix the flour, water and yeast until shaggy, rest 20 minutes, add the salt and knead for 8–10 minutes, until smooth.
  2. Cover and leave at room temperature for 2 hours, then refrigerate for 24 hours.
  3. Divide into 4 balls and let them rise at room temperature for 3–4 hours, covered.
  4. Put a pizza stone or steel on the top rack and heat the oven to its maximum (500–550 °F) for at least 45 minutes. Switch to the broiler for the last 5 minutes.
  5. Stretch each ball by hand into a 10-inch round, pressing the air towards the edge to form the rim. Do not use a rolling pin.
  6. Spread a thin layer of tomato, add the mozzarella and a drizzle of oil.
  7. Slide onto the hot stone and bake for 6–8 minutes, until the rim is puffed and blistered. Add basil and a little more oil as it comes out.
Tip. Use less topping than you think. In Naples, a good pizza is light enough to fold.
THE MICHELIN 3-STAR VERSION

Margherita, Three Tomatoes

Makes 4 pizzas · 3 days
A 72-hour cold-fermented dough, buffalo mozzarella stracciatella, three textures of tomato and basil oil, baked in a wood oven at 850 °F.

INGREDIENTS

  • Dough: 4 cups “00” Neapolitan flour, 1¾ cups cold water, 2 tsp sea salt, ⅛ tsp instant yeast (or 2 tbsp sourdough starter)
  • Raw sauce: 1 can (14 oz) DOP San Marzano tomatoes, passed through a food mill, salt
  • Confit: 1 cup Piennolo or cherry tomatoes, olive oil, garlic, thyme
  • Tomato water: clear juice from 2 lb ripe tomatoes, hung overnight
  • 8 oz buffalo mozzarella stracciatella
  • Basil oil: 1 large bunch basil, ½ cup olive oil
  • Flaky sea salt, aged Parmigiano

TECHNIQUE

  1. Make a very wet dough and cold-ferment it in the fridge for 72 hours, folding it twice on the first day.
  2. Ball the dough 6 hours before baking and leave at room temperature.
  3. Confit the tomatoes in olive oil with garlic and thyme at 250 °F for 1½ hours, until they wrinkle.
  4. Reduce the tomato water by half to a light, intensely flavored broth; cool.
  5. Blanch the basil for 5 seconds, ice it, blend with the oil and strain through a cloth.
  6. Stretch the dough, spread a thin layer of raw sauce and bake in a wood oven at 850 °F for 60–90 seconds (or as hot as your oven allows).
Straight from the oven: spoon on the cool stracciatella in small clouds, set the confit tomatoes between them, drizzle a few drops of reduced tomato water and basil oil, grate a little Parmigiano and finish with flaky salt and baby basil leaves.
