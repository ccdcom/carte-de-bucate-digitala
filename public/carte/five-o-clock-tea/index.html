Five O’Clock Tea

Scones with Clotted Cream
Rustic version ↓ Michelin version ↓

The story behind the dish

Afternoon tea is credited to Anna, Duchess of Bedford, who in the 1840s found the wait between lunch and an eight o’clock dinner unbearable and asked for tea, bread and butter and cake in her rooms. Her friends joined her, and soon Victorian society had a new ritual.

At its center are scones: split warm, spread with clotted cream and strawberry jam. In Devon the cream goes first, in Cornwall the jam, and the debate is still taken seriously.

THE RUSTIC VERSION

Scones for Tea

Makes 10 · 40 minutes
Light buttermilk scones with clotted cream and jam, the heart of a British tea.

INGREDIENTS

  • 3¼ cups all-purpose flour
  • 4 tsp baking powder
  • ¼ cup sugar and ½ tsp salt
  • 6 tbsp cold butter, cubed
  • 1 cup buttermilk (or whole milk), cold
  • 1 egg, beaten, to glaze
  • To serve: clotted cream (or mascarpone whipped with a little cream), strawberry jam, a pot of strong black tea

METHOD

  1. Heat the oven to 425 °F and line a baking sheet.
  2. Mix the flour, baking powder, sugar and salt. Rub in the butter until it looks like breadcrumbs.
  3. Add the buttermilk and mix quickly with a knife into a soft dough. Do not knead.
  4. Pat out to 1 inch thick and cut rounds with a 2½-inch cutter, pushing straight down without twisting.
  5. Brush the tops with egg and bake for 12–15 minutes, until risen and golden.
  6. Cool slightly in a towel. Serve warm, split by hand, with clotted cream and jam.
Tip. Twisting the cutter seals the edges and stops the scones rising tall.
THE MICHELIN 3-STAR VERSION

Afternoon Tea, Refined

Serves 4 · 3 hours
Warm brown-butter scones, Cornish clotted cream, strawberries in three textures and an Earl Grey cream, for a grand-hotel tea.

INGREDIENTS

  • Scones: as in the classic, with the butter browned and chilled first, and a little vanilla
  • Cornish clotted cream
  • Strawberries: fresh, macerated with a few drops of balsamic; a quick jam with vanilla; and freeze-dried strawberry powder
  • Earl Grey cream: 1 cup heavy cream infused with 2 Earl Grey tea bags, 2 tbsp sugar, whipped
  • Small sandwiches: cucumber with dill butter on white bread; smoked salmon on rye
  • Edible flowers, lemon zest

TECHNIQUE

  1. Infuse the cream with the tea overnight, strain and whip with the sugar.
  2. Make a quick jam with half the strawberries and vanilla; macerate the rest.
  3. Make the scones small (1½-inch) and bake just before serving.
  4. Prepare the finger sandwiches, crusts removed, cut precisely.
On a tiered stand: finger sandwiches at the bottom, warm scones in a linen napkin in the middle with clotted cream, jam and macerated strawberries, and at the top small cups of Earl Grey cream dusted with strawberry powder and flowers. Served with a pot of loose-leaf tea.
