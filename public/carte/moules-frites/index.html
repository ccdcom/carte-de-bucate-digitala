🇧🇪 Belgium

Moules-frites

Mussels and Fries
Rustic version ↓ Michelin version ↓

The story behind the dish

Mussels with fries is Belgium’s national dish: a steaming black pot of mussels cooked in their own juices with white wine or beer, and a cone of golden fries, twice-fried in beef fat, with mayonnaise on the side.

Mussels were the cheap food of the North Sea coast, and fries, Belgians insist, were invented in the Meuse valley, when villagers who could not fish in the frozen river fried potatoes cut like little fish. Together they became the Sunday treat of Brussels brasseries, best from September to April.

THE RUSTIC VERSION

The Brussels Brasserie Pot

Serves 2 generously · 1 hour
Mussels steamed with white wine, shallots and celery, and fries fried twice for crunch.

INGREDIENTS

  • 4 lb fresh mussels, scrubbed and debearded
  • 2 tbsp butter
  • 2 shallots and 1 celery stalk, finely chopped
  • 2 garlic cloves, chopped
  • 1 cup dry white wine (or Belgian blond beer)
  • A handful of chopped parsley, black pepper
  • Fries: 2 lb floury potatoes (Bintje or Russet), cut into ⅜-inch sticks, beef tallow or peanut oil for frying
  • Mayonnaise, to serve

METHOD

  1. Fries: soak the cut potatoes in cold water for 30 minutes, then dry very well. Fry at 300 °F for 6–7 minutes, until soft but pale. Drain and rest at least 20 minutes.
  2. Throw away any mussels that are cracked or stay open when tapped.
  3. In a large pot, melt the butter and soften the shallots, celery and garlic for 3 minutes.
  4. Add the wine and bring to a boil. Add the mussels, cover and cook over high heat for 4–6 minutes, shaking the pot, until they open.
  5. Meanwhile fry the potatoes again at 350 °F for 2–3 minutes, until golden and crisp. Salt.
  6. Sprinkle the mussels with parsley and pepper. Discard any that stay closed. Serve in the pot with the fries and mayonnaise, and use an empty shell as tongs.
THE MICHELIN 3-STAR VERSION

Mussel, Beer and Smoke

Serves 4 as a starter · 2 hours
Shelled mussels in a mussel-and-gueuze velouté, smoked mussels, crisp potato cylinders and a sea herb salad.

INGREDIENTS

  • 3 lb small mussels (Zeeland if possible)
  • 1 shallot, 1 celery stalk, thyme, 1 cup gueuze or white wine
  • Velouté: the mussel liquor, ½ cup cream, 2 tbsp butter, lemon juice, a pinch of saffron (optional)
  • 8 mussels, lightly smoked over beech chips for 5 minutes
  • Potatoes: 2 large potatoes, cut into neat cylinders, confit in tallow then fried crisp
  • Celery leaves, sea purslane, samphire
  • Mayonnaise with smoked oil, in dots

TECHNIQUE

  1. Steam the mussels open with the shallot, celery, thyme and gueuze. Shell most of them and strain the liquor through a cloth.
  2. Reduce the liquor by half, add the cream and simmer, then blend in the butter and a few drops of lemon for a light, foamy velouté.
  3. Smoke 8 shelled mussels briefly.
  4. Confit the potato cylinders in tallow at 250 °F until tender, then fry at 375 °F until crisp all over.
  5. Dress the sea herbs and celery leaves with a few drops of lemon.
A cluster of plump mussels and the smoked ones in a warm shallow bowl, crisp potato cylinders standing beside them, dots of smoked mayonnaise and the sea herbs. Froth the velouté with a hand blender and pour it around at the table.
