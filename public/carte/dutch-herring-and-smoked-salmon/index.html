🇳🇱 Netherlands

Dutch Herring and Smoked Salmon

Rustic version ↓ Michelin version ↓

The story behind the dish

Hollandse Nieuwe, the young herring of early summer, is the Netherlands at its most honest. Since the 14th century, when Willem Beukelszoon is said to have perfected gutting and salting herring at sea, the fish made the Dutch rich. Every June the first barrel is auctioned for charity and the season opens with flags on every fish stall.

The ritual is simple: hold the mild, silky herring by the tail, dip it in chopped onion, tip your head back and eat it in a few bites. Beside it, smoked fish, salmon and eel, carries on the old North Sea tradition of the smokehouse.

THE RUSTIC VERSION

From the Haring Stall

Serves 4 · 15 minutes
Young herring with onions and pickles, and smoked salmon on brown bread: a Dutch market lunch at home.

INGREDIENTS

  • 4 Hollandse Nieuwe (or the best mild matjes herring you can find), cleaned and filleted with tails
  • 1 small white onion, finely chopped
  • 4 dill pickles (augurken), sliced
  • 8 oz smoked salmon, thinly sliced
  • 4 slices dark whole-grain or rye bread, buttered
  • Lemon wedges, dill, horseradish cream
  • Soft white rolls (puntjes) for broodje haring

METHOD

  1. Keep the herring very cold until the last moment.
  2. Lay each herring on a plate with a little heap of onion and pickle slices.
  3. Eat it the Dutch way: hold by the tail, dip in onion, and eat from the head end down. Or cut into pieces and serve on a cocktail stick.
  4. For a broodje haring, split a soft roll, add two herring fillets, onions and pickles.
  5. Arrange the smoked salmon on buttered brown bread with dill, a squeeze of lemon and a spoon of horseradish cream.
Tip. Raw herring must have been frozen first to be safe; in the Netherlands this is required by law, and good fishmongers elsewhere do the same.
THE MICHELIN 3-STAR VERSION

North Sea on a Plate

Serves 4 as a starter · 1½ hours
Herring tartare with green apple and beet, cold-smoked salmon with buttermilk and dill, rye crisp and pickled onion.

INGREDIENTS

  • 4 Hollandse Nieuwe herrings, finely diced
  • ½ Granny Smith apple and 1 small roasted beet, in tiny dice
  • 1 shallot, pickled in white wine vinegar and sugar
  • 8 oz cold-smoked salmon in one piece, sliced thick
  • Buttermilk dressing: ½ cup buttermilk, 1 tbsp dill oil, lemon, salt
  • Rye crisps: thin slices of rye bread, baked crisp
  • Horseradish snow (frozen horseradish, grated), dill, borage flowers, salmon roe

TECHNIQUE

  1. Mix the herring with the apple, beet and a little pickled shallot. Season with lemon.
  2. Bake the rye slices at 300 °F for 20 minutes, until crisp.
  3. Season the buttermilk with dill oil, lemon and salt; it should break slightly into green droplets.
  4. Cut the salmon into neat thick slices and brush with a little oil.
A quenelle of herring tartare on a rye crisp, a slice of salmon folded beside it in a pool of split buttermilk dressing, salmon roe, pickled shallot rings, dill, borage flowers, and fresh horseradish grated over at the table.
