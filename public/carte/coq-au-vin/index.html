Coq au Vin

Wine-Braised Chicken
Rustic version ↓ Michelin version ↓

The story behind the dish

Legend links coq au vin to Julius Caesar and the Gauls, but the dish is really a farmhouse answer to an old rooster too tough to roast: braise it slowly in red Burgundy wine until tender. Lardons, small onions and mushrooms complete the classic garnish.

It became a symbol of French bistro cooking in the 20th century, and Julia Child brought it to America in 1961. Today it is usually made with a good chicken, and every Burgundian cook knows it is even better the next day.

THE RUSTIC VERSION

Burgundy Farmhouse Braise

Serves 6 · 3 hours, plus overnight marinating
Chicken marinated overnight in red wine, then slowly braised with bacon, onions and mushrooms.

INGREDIENTS

  • 1 large chicken (about 4½ lb), cut into 8 pieces, or 8 thighs and drumsticks
  • 1 bottle (3 cups) red Burgundy or Pinot Noir
  • 1 onion, 1 carrot, 2 garlic cloves, thyme, bay leaf, for the marinade
  • 6 oz smoked bacon, in lardons
  • 2 tbsp butter and 1 tbsp oil
  • 2 tbsp flour
  • 1 tbsp tomato paste and 2 tbsp Cognac
  • 1 cup chicken stock
  • 12 small pearl onions, peeled
  • 8 oz button mushrooms
  • Chopped parsley; boiled potatoes or crusty bread to serve

METHOD

  1. Marinate the chicken with the wine, sliced vegetables and herbs overnight in the fridge.
  2. Lift out the chicken and pat dry; strain the wine and keep the vegetables.
  3. Brown the lardons in a heavy pot, remove. Brown the chicken in the fat with the butter and oil, in batches; remove.
  4. Soften the marinade vegetables, stir in the flour and tomato paste, add the Cognac, then the wine and stock. Return the chicken and lardons.
  5. Cover and simmer gently (or bake at 325 °F) for 1½ hours, until very tender.
  6. Meanwhile brown the pearl onions and mushrooms in butter.
  7. Lift out the chicken, reduce the sauce until it coats a spoon, return the chicken, add the onions and mushrooms and simmer 10 minutes. Sprinkle with parsley.
THE MICHELIN 3-STAR VERSION

Coq au Vin, Reduced and Refined

Serves 4 · 1 day
A roasted free-range chicken breast and braised thigh roulade, a glossy red wine sauce, glazed onions, morels and pommes purée.

INGREDIENTS

  • 1 free-range chicken (Bresse if possible): 2 breasts on the skin, 4 thighs deboned
  • Sauce: 1 bottle red Burgundy, reduced by two-thirds with shallots and thyme, 2 cups dark chicken jus, 2 tbsp cold butter
  • 4 oz smoked pork belly, cut in fine batons and crisped
  • 12 pearl onions, glazed with butter, sugar and a little stock
  • 8 fresh or rehydrated morels, sautéed
  • Pommes purée: 1 lb ratte or Yukon Gold potatoes, ½ cup butter, ⅓ cup warm milk
  • Parsley oil, thyme flowers

TECHNIQUE

  1. Braise the deboned thighs in a little of the wine reduction and jus at 300 °F for 2 hours. Shred, mix with some sauce, roll tightly in plastic wrap into a cylinder and chill.
  2. Combine the wine reduction and jus and reduce to a glossy sauce. Finish with cold butter.
  3. Make a very smooth potato purée, passing it through a sieve and beating in the butter and milk.
  4. Roast the breasts skin side down in a pan until golden, then finish at 350 °F to 150 °F internal. Rest.
  5. Slice the roulade into thick rounds and sear both sides.
