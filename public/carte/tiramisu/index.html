Tiramisu

Classic Italian Coffee Dessert
Rustic version ↓ Michelin version ↓

The story behind the dish

Despite its fame, tiramisu is young. Most food historians trace it to Treviso in the late 1960s, to the restaurant Le Beccherie, where it grew from an old Venetian habit of reviving the tired with egg yolks beaten with sugar. Its name means “pick me up.”

Ladyfingers soaked in espresso, a cream of mascarpone and eggs, a dusting of bitter cocoa: it took only a few decades to become the world’s favorite Italian dessert, with endless variations from berries to pistachio.

THE RUSTIC VERSION

The Treviso Classic

Serves 8 · 30 minutes, plus 6 hours chilling
No cream, no gelatin: eggs, sugar, mascarpone, coffee and cocoa, as it was first made.

INGREDIENTS

  • 4 very fresh eggs, separated
  • ½ cup sugar
  • 1 lb mascarpone, cold
  • About 30 Savoiardi (ladyfingers)
  • 1½ cups strong espresso, cooled (add 2 tbsp Marsala or rum if you like)
  • Unsweetened cocoa powder for dusting

METHOD

  1. Beat the yolks with the sugar for 5 minutes, until thick and pale.
  2. Add the mascarpone and beat briefly, just until smooth.
  3. In a clean bowl, beat the whites to firm peaks and fold them gently into the mascarpone mixture.
  4. Dip each ladyfinger very quickly in the coffee (one second each side) and lay them in a single layer in a 9 × 13-inch dish.
  5. Spread half of the cream over them. Repeat with another layer of dipped ladyfingers and the rest of the cream.
  6. Cover and refrigerate for at least 6 hours, ideally overnight.
  7. Dust generously with cocoa just before serving.
Tip. Use pasteurized eggs if you are serving young children, pregnant guests or the elderly.
THE MICHELIN 3-STAR VERSION

Tiramisu, Coffee in Five Ways

Serves 6 · 4 hours
Mascarpone sabayon cream, espresso-soaked sponge, coffee granita, a crisp of cocoa and a coffee caramel, assembled at the table.

INGREDIENTS

  • Sabayon cream: 4 egg yolks, ⅓ cup sugar, 2 tbsp Marsala, 9 oz mascarpone, ½ cup heavy cream, whipped
  • Sponge: 3 eggs, ⅓ cup sugar, ½ cup flour (or 18 homemade ladyfingers)
  • Espresso soak: 1 cup espresso, 2 tbsp sugar, 1 tbsp coffee liqueur
  • Granita: 1 cup espresso, ¼ cup sugar
  • Coffee caramel: ½ cup sugar, ¼ cup espresso, 2 tbsp cream, pinch of salt
  • Cocoa tuile: 2 tbsp butter, 2 tbsp sugar, 1 egg white, 2 tbsp flour, 1 tbsp cocoa
  • Cocoa powder, cocoa nibs

TECHNIQUE

  1. Granita: dissolve the sugar in the espresso, freeze flat and scrape every 30 minutes until flaky.
  2. Sabayon: whisk the yolks, sugar and Marsala over simmering water to 160 °F, until thick. Cool, beat in the mascarpone, then fold in the whipped cream. Pipe into a bag and chill.
  3. Bake the sponge in a thin sheet at 375 °F for 8 minutes and cut into small cubes.
  4. Caramel: cook the sugar to amber, add the espresso and cream carefully and simmer until smooth. Add salt.
  5. Tuile: mix all ingredients, spread very thin, bake at 340 °F for 6 minutes.
In a chilled glass bowl, a few cubes of sponge soaked at the last moment in espresso, generous rosettes of sabayon cream, a few drops of coffee caramel, a spoon of granita, cocoa nibs, a cocoa tuile standing up, and cocoa sifted over the top at the table.
